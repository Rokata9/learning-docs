Draw a translucent rounded panel

The background will remain visible through the panel. Its colors will become lighter inside the panel. Its lines, circles, and letters will not be blurred or bent.

1 · The main graphics idea

One foreground fill changes the colors beneath it

The alpha-compositing formula is postponed until Lesson 1.6.

Panel

A panel is the foreground rectangular area added over the study background. This lesson's panel has no text or icons.

Concrete object: the empty Box produced by TranslucentRoundedPanel().

Bounds

Bounds are the width, height, and position available to one drawn object.

Concrete size: 328.dp × 190.dp. dp means density-independent pixel.

Fill

A fill is the color drawn inside a shape's boundary.

Concrete fill: white. The fill does not read, move, or soften background pixels.

Opacity

Opacity is how strongly the foreground color appears. Compose code often calls this value alpha.

Course value: 0.25f, which means 25% opacity.

Rounded shape: RoundedCornerShape(32.dp) tells Compose to round the fill boundary. The geometry behind that corner value is taught in Chapter 4. For now, treat 32.dp as the chosen rounding amount.

4 · Diagnose sharpness

Lower contrast is not the same as blur

The numbers here are illustrative teaching units. They are not Android's internal pixel weights.

Original line width20 teaching units
Width after translucency20 teaching units
What actually changesThe line and its neighboring background both become lighter. The two line boundaries stay at the same positions.
Diagnostic rule for this lesson: a feature can look less prominent because its contrast changed. Call it blur only when the boundary spreads over nearby positions and becomes spatially wider.

package com.example.liquidglasslab.chapter01

import androidx.compose.foundation.background
import androidx.compose.foundation.layout.Box
import androidx.compose.foundation.shape.RoundedCornerShape
import androidx.compose.runtime.Composable
import androidx.compose.ui.Modifier
import androidx.compose.ui.graphics.Color
import androidx.compose.ui.unit.dp

internal const val PanelFillAlpha = 0.25f

private val PanelShape = RoundedCornerShape(32.dp)

@Composable
internal fun TranslucentRoundedPanel(
    modifier: Modifier = Modifier,
) {
    Box(
        modifier = modifier.background(
            color = Color.White.copy(alpha = PanelFillAlpha),
            shape = PanelShape,
        ),
    )
}

10 · Final exercises
Answer before opening the hidden answers
The first question targets the most likely misconception.

    
Exercise 1 · Diagnostic: A black line under the panel looks lighter and less prominent. Is that enough evidence to call the result blur? Explain.
Answer: no. The white translucent fill changes the line's color and its contrast against neighboring colors. Blur requires the line boundary to spread into nearby positions. In this lesson, the boundary stays at the same position and width.

    
Exercise 2 · Why does StudyBackground() appear before TranslucentRoundedPanel() inside the parent Box?
Answer: the study background must occupy the scene behind the panel. The panel is emitted afterward so it is the foreground child in this scene. Reversing the order would let the opaque study background cover the panel.

    
Exercise 3 · What visible result should PanelFillAlpha = 0f produce? What should PanelFillAlpha = 1f produce?
Answer: at 0f, the white fill contributes nothing visible, so the panel area looks unchanged. At 1f, the white fill is fully opaque, so the background inside the rounded shape is hidden by white.

    
Exercise 4 · Name three important Liquid Glass behaviors that this panel still does not implement.
Answer: any three of real backdrop blur or diffusion, refraction or displaced sampling, adaptive tint, edge lighting, shadow, touch deformation, 3D perspective, or shape morphing. This lesson adds only a translucent white rounded fill.
