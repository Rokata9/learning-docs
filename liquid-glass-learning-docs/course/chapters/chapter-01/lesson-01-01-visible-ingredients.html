Chapter 1 · Lesson 1.1

Name the visible ingredients

A modern glass surface is not one effect. It is several visible operations working together. This lesson lets you turn those operations on one at a time.

Do not try to memorize an implementation. First learn what each operation looks like. Later lessons explain how iOS produces each result.

Lesson goal

After this lesson, you should be able to look at a surface and say:

  • the background is only mixed with a foreground color;
  • the background is softened;
  • the background is shifted or bent;
  • the edge is brighter or darker;
  • the shape itself is changing.
1 · Where this lesson fits

Observe before implementation

New graphics idea: a glass appearance contains several separate operations.
New math idea: none.

Visible fact

A visible fact describes pixels or shape changes that you can point to in an image or recording.

Example: “The straight line looks displaced inside the panel.”

Implementation hypothesis

An implementation hypothesis is a possible explanation for how software produced the visible result.

Example: “The app may sample the background from shifted positions.” This would need documentation, source code, tracing, profiling, or measurement to prove.

2 · Terms

Eight words for eight visible clues

These are first-pass definitions. Later chapters make each model more precise.

Translucency

The background remains visible while a foreground material also adds color.

Clue: colors change, but sharp edges can remain sharp.

Blur

Nearby background positions contribute to one final pixel.

Clue: thin edges spread into soft bands.

Diffusion

Light appears to spread inside the material. User interfaces often imitate this with blur and other softening operations.

Clue: fine detail becomes quieter.

Refraction

Background detail appears shifted, bent, stretched, or magnified.

Clue: a straight line changes position or shape.

Tint

The material adds a controlled color influence.

Clue: many colors move toward one added hue.

Highlight

A bright region suggests reflected light. A concentrated bright reflection is called a specular highlight.

Clue: one edge or spot is brighter.

Shadow

A darker region outside or below a surface suggests separation from the background.

Clue: the panel appears raised.

Morph

One shape changes continuously into another shape.

Clue: width, height, corners, or outline change without a sudden replacement.

9 · Final exercises

Answer in chat before opening the solutions

These questions test the likely confusion between translucency, blur, and refraction.

Exercise 1

A white panel makes the background paler. Thin text edges remain sharp. Which visible ingredient is definitely present?

Exercise 2 · Diagnostic

Can increasing only a panel’s opacity bend a straight line behind the panel? Explain why.

Exercise 3

A line appears shifted sideways inside a rounded surface. Which ingredient does that clue suggest most strongly?

Exercise 4

Classify this statement: “The menu probably copies the background into a separate image before drawing.” Is it a visible fact or an implementation hypothesis? What evidence would improve confidence?

Write all four answers before copying.
Hidden answer — Exercise 1

Translucency is definitely present. The foreground white changes the background colors. The sharp edges do not prove blur.

Hidden answer — Exercise 2

No. Opacity changes how strongly foreground and background colors mix at the same position. Bending requires a different operation that reads or maps background detail from another position.

Hidden answer — Exercise 3

Refraction or another form of displaced sampling is the strongest clue. The lesson does not yet prove which implementation produced it.

Hidden answer — Exercise 4

It is an implementation hypothesis. Stronger evidence could come from official documentation, framework or app source code, graphics tracing, or controlled measurement. Watching the pixels alone does not prove the specific implementation.
