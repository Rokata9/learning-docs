Chapter 6 · final architecture lesson

Build a two-layer scene architecture

Use two controlled GraphicsLayer objects for the backdrop source and the panel sample. Draw tint, border, and sharp foreground content in explicit later stages.

Main rule: the future blur layer must own the backdrop crop, but it must not own the label or icon that should remain sharp.

Two layers does not mean two pixel buffers

Layer 1 retains the complete backdrop drawing commands. Layer 2 retains the translated panel-crop commands.

ForegroundContent is an ordinary later Compose child, not a third layer.

Both controlled layers stay on the default Auto strategy in this lesson. Chapter 7 will add the operation that actually requires pixel input.

1 · Practical problem

Keep the future blur away from labels

Architecture decides which commands can become effect input.

One overloaded panel owner

If the sampled backdrop, tint, border, icon, and label all enter one future blur layer, the sharp foreground becomes effect input too.

One explicit owner split

Let GlassMaterial own the sampled backdrop and material additions. Let ForegroundContent draw afterward as an ordinary child.

Lessons 6.6–6.10 established background recording, crop mapping, freshness, one-way ownership, synchronized movement, and the difference between a command source and a destination raster target. This lesson assembles those prerequisites into one readable production route.

9 · Explain back

Four checks for the architecture

Open an answer only after stating your own version.

Why is ForegroundContent not a third layer?

The architecture needs only two controlled replay boundaries: the full backdrop source and the panel crop. The foreground is ordinary later Compose content. Adding a third layer would not improve the ownership rule being taught.

Why will the label stay outside a future effect on panelSampleLayer?

The label command is created after ArchitectureGlassMaterial. It never runs inside panelSampleLayer.record, so it is not part of that effect owner's input.

Why can the panel not capture itself?

BackdropHost and GlassPanel are siblings. Only the backdrop sibling is recorded into backgroundLayer; the consumer panel belongs to a different, later subtree.

Why does one panel position also define the crop in this sample?

BackdropHost fills ParentScene, and both begin at local origin (0, 0). Therefore the panel's parent-local origin equals its backdrop-local crop origin. Arbitrary nested layouts still require a real coordinate mapping.
