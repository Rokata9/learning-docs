Light, surface direction, and material thickness

Make the edge of the glass behave like a shaped surface, not a flat white border. The chapter gives every pixel a height, then a slope and a surface direction, and then lets light react to that direction.

The host stays the Chapter 8 path: a .metal file, ShaderLibrary, and ShaderRect colorEffect. Every lesson ends with a Metal exercise: you write real Metal functions, and the lab has the calling code and a check ready.

1 · Purpose and prerequisites

From a flat mask to a shaped surface

Chapter 9 answered two questions for every pixel: is it glass (the mask), and where does it read (the push). Chapter 10 adds a third: how is the surface shaped there?

What you already have

Earlier lessons supply the right triangle and length, signed distance, rounded rectangles and capsules, clamp, the lens, and the surface height.

Where the chapter goes

Height (10.1) → slope (10.2) → surface direction (10.3) → the dot product (10.4) → diffuse light and a highlight (10.5, 10.6) → edge effects (10.7–10.9) → one thickness value that drives several properties (10.10–10.12) → material shader version 1 (10.13).

3 · Vocabulary

Words this chapter adds

Surface height

h: how high the glass stands above one pixel, in UV, the same unit as positions. Not the height of a box (Lesson 2.4). The screen stays flat; h is a number per pixel.

Bevel and rim circle

The bevel is the rounded band along the outline, bevel = 0.10 wide. Its side view is a quarter of the rim circle: center K, bevel inside the outline, radius bevel. So the flat top is bevel high.

Height map

The picture that stores gray = h ÷ bevel for every pixel: black at height 0, white on the flat top. It is a way to see the heights; later lessons use h itself.

X slope

A height change divided by horizontal distance: slopeX = (hRight − hLeft) ÷ (2 × step). Plus rises to the right; minus falls. The result is the average slope between the two neighbors, an estimate at W.

Surface normal

An arrow that starts on the surface and stands at a square corner to it, pointing out of the glass. Turning each slope triangle a quarter turn gives rawNormal = (−slopeX, −slopeY, 1), with slopeY = (hE − hD) ÷ (2 × step) along y. Its x and y parts lie along the screen; the third part is the height direction, toward your eye.

Unit normal and normal-map RGB

N = rawNormal ÷ normalLength has length 1; normalLength = √(x² + y² + z²) is Lesson 9.1's triangle used twice. The display maps each member with RGB = N × 0.5 + 0.5. That color shows direction; it is not lighting.

HeightMap.metal

Lesson 10.2. slopeMap reads Lesson 10.1 heights one step left and right, then computes slopeX = (hRight − hLeft) ÷ (2 × step). Explorer: SlopeExplorer.swift, with step and bevel sliders, a side view, and the full trace.

SlopeExercise.metal

The Lesson 10.2 Metal exercise: roundedRectHeightForSlope and roundedRectSlopeX. SlopeExerciseKey.metal is the answer key; SlopeExerciseView.swift reuses markDifference for the picture check.

NormalMap.metal

Lesson 10.3. normalMap reads four heights, computes slopeX and slopeY, builds rawNormal = (−slopeX, −slopeY, 1), and stores the unit normal as N × 0.5 + 0.5. Explorer: NormalExplorer.swift.

NormalExercise.metal

The Lesson 10.3 Metal exercise: unitNormalFromSlopes and buttonNormal (the button height buttonHeightForNormal is prepared). NormalExerciseKey.metal is the answer key; NormalExerciseView.swift reuses markDifference.
