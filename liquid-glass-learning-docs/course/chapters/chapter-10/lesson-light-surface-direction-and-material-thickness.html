Light, surface direction, and material thickness

Make the edge of the glass behave like a shaped surface, not a flat white border. The chapter gives every pixel a height, then a slope and a surface direction, and then lets light react to that direction.

The host stays the Chapter 8 path: a .metal file, ShaderLibrary, and ShaderRect colorEffect. The other lessons teach one idea each, in pictures.

From a flat mask to a shaped surface

3 · Vocabulary

Words this chapter adds

Surface height

h: how high the glass stands above one pixel, in UV, the same unit as positions. Not the height of a box (Lesson 2.4). The screen stays flat; h is a number per pixel.

Bevel and rim circle

The bevel is the rounded band along the outline, bevel = 0.10 wide. Its side view is a quarter of the rim circle: center K, bevel inside the outline, radius bevel. So the flat top is bevel high.

Height map

The picture that stores gray = h ÷ bevel for every pixel: black at height 0, white on the flat top. It is a way to see the heights; later lessons use h itself.

X slope

A height change divided by horizontal distance: slopeX = (hRight − hLeft) ÷ (2 × step). Plus rises to the right; minus falls. The result is the average slope between the two neighbors, an estimate at W.

Surface normal

An arrow that starts on the surface and stands at a square corner to it, pointing out of the glass. Turning each slope triangle a quarter turn gives rawNormal = (−slopeX, −slopeY, 1), with slopeY = (hE − hD) ÷ (2 × step) along y. Its x and y parts lie along the screen; the third part is the height direction, toward your eye.

Dot product

dot(N, L): how far N goes along L, with both arrows 1 long. Draw a line from the tip of N to the line of L, at a square corner. The number is the distance from the start to where it meets that line; a minus means behind the start. Multiply each part of N by the same part of L, then add. 1: the same direction. 0: a square corner. −1: opposite.

Unit normal and normal-map RGB

N = rawNormal ÷ normalLength has length 1; normalLength = √(x² + y² + z²) is Lesson 9.1's triangle used twice. The display maps each member with RGB = N × 0.5 + 0.5. That color shows direction; it is not lighting.
