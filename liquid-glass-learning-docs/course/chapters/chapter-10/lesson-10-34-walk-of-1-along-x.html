Lesson 10.34 — A walk of 1 along x moves the meeting point to L's x part

Light theme · desktop · works from file://.

Here the flip of Lessons 10.27–10.30 finds where a walk of 1 along x moves the meeting point on L's line.

Where this is used. Lessons 10.25–10.37 build, step by step, the formula for how far N goes along L, written dot(N, L). dot(N, L) sets each pixel's brightness (Lesson 10.41) and where the highlight sits (Lessons 10.47–10.51); Lesson 10.46 computes it in Metal.

Uses

  • Lessons 10.28–10.30: the flip over the gray line swaps the x line and L's line, mark on mark, and keeps square corners.
  • Lesson 10.16: the foot of an arrow's tip on the x line reads its x part; Lesson 10.18: the same on the height line.
  • Lesson 10.19: L = (0.48, −0.60, 0.64); a point has one meeting point on L's line. Lesson 10.24: a point's meeting point, where the line from it meets L's line at 90°; a walk moves the meeting point from S to the meeting point of its end.

In a calculation, green marks a number known before the calculation starts: given, or found earlier. The other numbers are worked out in it.

Exercise 1 — How far along L₂'s line does a walk of 1 along x move the meeting point?

How far along L₂'s line does a walk of 1 along x move the meeting point?

Show the answer
  • The foot Q of the tip of L₂ on the x line reads 0.80, L₂'s x part (Lesson 10.16).
  • The flip takes the tip of L₂ onto X and Q 0.80 along L₂'s line, as Q′, with its square corner: XQ′ meets L₂'s line at 90° at Q′, so Q′ is X's meeting point J (Lesson 10.19).
  • So the walk of 1 along x moves the meeting point to 0.80.
