Lesson 10.43 — Facing away: max(dot, 0)

Light theme · desktop · works from file://.

Why: Lesson 10.42 found brightness = dot(N, L). But at Z = (0.33, 0.67), on the lower left of the rim, dot(N, L) = −0.45284. A brightness is a share of the light (beam width ÷ lit length, Lesson 10.41), so it cannot be below 0. This lesson finds the brightness at Z.

Where this is used. Lesson 10.45 gives the pixels with brightness 0 a little light, so they are not black. Lesson 10.47 puts max(dot(N, L), 0) into the shader of the lit glass.

Uses

  • Lesson 10.42: brightness at S = dot(N, L).
  • Lesson 10.41: brightness at S = beam width ÷ lit length; the rays of light are parallel to L's line; near S the glass is a small board at 90° to N, and N points out of the glass (Lesson 10.3).
  • Lesson 10.20: when dot(N, L) is below 0, F is on the minus side of L's line. Lesson 10.22: when the angle between N and L is 90°, F is at S.
  • Lesson 10.39: the dot map, with its red line where dot(N, L) = 0.
  • Lesson 9.6: max(a, b), the larger of a and b.
