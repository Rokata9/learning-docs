Lesson 10.6 — A turn around the x line keeps a line on the wall

Turn a straight line on the wall around the x line: it stays on the wall. Light theme · desktop · works from file://.

In Lesson 10.5 the wall of Lesson 10.4 turned out flat: the straight line between two of its points stays on it. This lesson draws one straight line on that wall, from Gx, and turns it around the x line: the turn keeps it on the wall. Lesson 10.8 shows that the turn keeps its length; Lessons 10.10 and 10.11 use the turn to reach every line on the wall through Gx.

New here
  • The teal line and its end T: a straight line on the wall from Gx. Step 2.
  • The spin, by definition: a turn around the x line, like a wheel on its axle: every point of the teal line keeps how far to the right of S it is and changes only its y and height. Step 3.
  • The one idea: so the spin keeps the teal line on the wall (Step 3).

Exercise 1 — Can any spin bring T to point a?

Why this question. Point a is 0.953 up from Gx's level, as high as T after a spin of 90°, but 0.2 to the right of Gx (red in the picture). Can the spin get T there?

What you do. Read how far to the right of Gx point a is. Then remember what the spin keeps (Step 3).

Question: can any spin bring T to point a?

Show the answer
  • The spin changes only y and height, so T is always 0.302 to the right of S: on the wall.
  • Point a: 0.302 + 0.2 = 0.502 to the right of S, off the wall.
  • At 90°, T is straight up from Gx, 0.953 up like point a, but 0.2 to its left.
  • So no spin brings T to point a.
Exercise 2 — The teal line has spun 150°. How far to the right of S is T now?

Why this question. The spin has moved T far toward the top of the picture and up. Has it also moved T to the right or to the left?

What you do. Remember which numbers the spin changes (Step 3). Then read where T is.

Question: the teal line has spun 150°. How far to the right of S is T now?

Show the answer
  • The spin changed only T's y and height.
  • So T stayed on the wall, seen edge-on as a line in this view.
  • So T is 0.302 to the right of S, like Gx.
