Lesson 10.12 — A wider angle at Gx puts T farther from S

Light theme · desktop · works from file://.

|SGx| and |GxT| stay fixed. Open ∠SGxT, and |ST| grows. So |ST| tells the angle. Lesson 10.15 uses this.

Uses

  • Lesson 10.4: the x leg SGx, |SGx| = 0.302. Lesson 10.6: the teal line GxT, |GxT| = 0.953.
  • Lesson 9.1: a length from its parts, √(x part² + y part²).

Exercise 1 — At W = (0.71, 0.50), |ST| is 0.719. Is ∠SGxT more than 90°, or less?

At W = (0.71, 0.50) in UV, N = (0.605, 0, 0.796) . So |SGx| = N's x part = 0.605 (Lesson 10.4), and the teal line is 0.796 long . At 90°, |ST| = 1.000 (drawn).

The hinge at Gx has opened or closed, and now |ST| = 0.719. Lesson 10.15 reads an angle from |ST| in this way.

At W = (0.71, 0.50), |ST| is 0.719. Is ∠SGxT more than 90°, or less?

Show the answer
  • At 90°, |ST| = 1.000 (faint).
  • A narrower angle moves T along its path toward S, onto smaller circles around S; a wider one, onto bigger circles.
  • |ST| = 0.719 is less than 1.000, so ∠SGxT is less than 90°: here T is at 60° (click T for its chain).
