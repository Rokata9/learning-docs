Lesson 10.7 — The wall seen straight on draws its lines at their whole length

A straight line on the wall is drawn exactly as long as it is; a line with an x part is drawn shorter. Light theme · desktop · works from file://.

Lessons 10.4 to 10.6 drew the wall seen straight on. This lesson shows why that drawing shows every straight line on the wall at its whole length, and why N, which has an x part, is drawn shorter. Lesson 10.8 uses it to measure the teal line while it spins.

New here
  • The one idea: the wall seen straight on draws every straight line on the wall from Gx at its whole length, because it copies the line's y leg and height leg at full size and at a square corner (Step 4).
  • Its limit: a line with an x part, like N, is drawn shorter (Step 5).
  • Drawn length: how long a line looks in a drawing. Its length is Lesson 10.3's, from its parts. A line is drawn at its whole length when the two are equal.

Exercise 1 — Which of the lines a, b and c from Gx does the wall seen straight on draw at its whole length?

Why this question. The wall seen straight on draws only two of each point's three numbers. Some lines lose nothing that way; others do.

What you do. Read each line's three parts in the table. Ask which part the drawing leaves out (Step 2), and whether that line has such a part.

Question: which of the lines a, b and c from Gx does the wall seen straight on draw at its whole length?

Show the answer
  • Line a: x part 0, on the wall. Drawn and real length are the same:
    • y part² = (−0.5) × (−0.5) = 0.25
    • height part² = 0.5 × 0.5 = 0.25
    • length² = 0.25 + 0.25 = 0.5
    • length = √0.5 = 0.707
  • Line b: x part 0.3, which is not drawn.
    • Drawn, from its y part and height part:
      • y part² = 0.4 × 0.4 = 0.16
      • height part² = 0.5 × 0.5 = 0.25
      • drawn length² = 0.16 + 0.25 = 0.41
      • drawn length = √0.41 = 0.640
    • Real, from all three parts (Lesson 10.3: first the shadow):
      • x part² = 0.3 × 0.3 = 0.09
      • shadow² = x part² + y part² = 0.09 + 0.16 = 0.25
      • length² = shadow² + height part² = 0.25 + 0.25 = 0.5
      • length = √0.5 = 0.707
  • Line c: only an x part, 0.5. It is drawn as the point Gx: 0 long.
  • So only line a is drawn at its whole length.
Exercise 2 — In the wall seen straight on, line d from S is drawn 0.4 long. Its x part is 0.3. How long is line d?

Why this question. The drawing shows line d 0.4 long. Line d has an x part, so that is not all of it.

What you do. Take the drawn length and the x part that the drawing leaves out. Step 5 says how they make the length.

Question: in the wall seen straight on, line d from S is drawn 0.4 long. Its x part is 0.3. How long is line d?

Show the answer
  • Drawn: only line d's y part and height part:
    • y part² = 0.24 × 0.24 = 0.0576
    • height part² = 0.32 × 0.32 = 0.1024
    • drawn length² = 0.0576 + 0.1024 = 0.16
    • drawn length = √0.16 = 0.4
  • Real: add the x part, as in Step 5:
    • x part² = 0.3 × 0.3 = 0.09
    • length² = x part² + drawn length² = 0.09 + 0.16 = 0.25
    • length = √0.25 = 0.5
  • So line d is 0.5 long. It is drawn shorter, 0.4, because its x part is not drawn.
