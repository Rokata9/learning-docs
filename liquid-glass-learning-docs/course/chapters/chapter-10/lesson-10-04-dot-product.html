Lesson 10.4 — Dot product

Measure how much two arrows point the same way, as one number. Light theme · desktop · works from file://.

Lesson 10.3 gave every pixel an arrow, N, that says which way the surface faces. This lesson compares N with a second arrow, L, the direction toward a light. The comparison gives one number per pixel: 1 when the two arrows point the same way, 0 at a square corner, −1 when they point opposite ways. Lesson 10.5 turns that number into brightness.

Section 2 · one value, traced to the endW gets the number 0.7994

  • The pixel changes here: from K to W = (0.71, 0.50). The coordinates stay UV, from the picture's top-left corner, y down.
  • The glass is the glass of Lesson 10.3: C = (0.50, 0.50), radius 0.25, bevel 0.10, step 0.01.
  • Every pixel has its own N.
  • L is the same at every pixel. Why? We treat the light as very far away, like the sun.
  • So every pixel gets one number, dot(N, L). The dot map stores it as gray.

Drag W, the white dot, or the tip of L. Click a numbered point: it says its number and why.

  • Each pixel stores gray = dot(N, L) × 0.5 + 0.5. This is only for seeing the number.
  • Flat top and outside: N = (0, 0, 1). So the number is L's height part, 0.64, and every pixel there stores gray 0.82.
  • On the rim N leans, so the number depends on which way N leans:
    • N leans the way L leans (point 8): almost 1, almost white;
    • N leans the other way (point 4, and nearer the outline): below 0, dark.
  • This is a number, not light yet. Lesson 10.5 decides what the glass does with it.

Section 3 · short code excerptThe iPhone shader

The real code is in chapter10/DotMap.metal. The helper is Lesson 10.3's N. The new lines compare it with L and show the result as gray.

// Lesson 10.3's N at one UV position.
static float3 circleNormalForDot(float2 uv, float2 center, float radius, float bevel, float step) { … }

[[ stitchable ]] half4 dotMap(
    float2 position, half4 color, float2 resolution, float2 center,
    float radius, float bevel, float step, float3 L, float scale
) {
    float2 uv = position * scale / resolution;
    float3 N = circleNormalForDot(uv, center, radius, bevel, step);
    float dotNL = N.x * L.x + N.y * L.y + N.z * L.z;
    float gray = dotNL * 0.5 + 0.5;
    return half4(gray, gray, gray, 1.0);
}

float dotOfArrows(float3 a, float3 b) {
    return a.x * b.x + a.y * b.y + a.z * b.z;
}

float buttonDot(float2 uv, float2 center, float2 outerHalf, float cornerRadius,
                float bevel, float step, float3 light) {
    float lightLength = sqrt(dotOfArrows(light, light));
    float3 L = light / lightLength;
    float3 N = buttonNormalForDot(uv, center, outerHalf, cornerRadius, bevel, step);
    return dotOfArrows(N, L);
}

The GPU keeps every digit of N. It gets −0.356179 and gray 0.321911 at T6. The last digits differ here because these lines round N to six decimals.

Section 8 · your questionsLevel, the three parts, F at the tip of N, minus, and Step 6's triangles

Your questions about this lesson, with their answers. The rest of the page does not need this section. Your questions quote the old Section 1, which used the tilted board and its middle point P. Section 1 now uses the glass at the pixel K = (0.62, 0.66) in UV, with S, the glass above K, in place of P. Every card uses K and S.

1 · What “level” means

“"The x line is level, so it leaves the tilted board." - does this mean it's parallel to the bottom ground?”

  • Yes. Level means parallel to the floor. The floor is the screen, at height 0.
  • So every point of the x line has the same height.
  • The x line goes through S, so that height is S's height: 0.0866 in UV (lime dots).
  • The glass along the line y = 0.66 is not level. It falls as x grows, toward the outline of the glass (violet dots).
  • So the glass is above the x line on one side of S and below it on the other side. That is all “leaves” meant.
  • Step 2 now says: “The x line is level: parallel to the floor, at S's height.”

Heights in UV, 4 decimals, because they are small. Each ⓘ gives the glass height's chain (Lesson 10.1).

2 · N's y part and height part, drawn

“"The y part and the height part say the same for y and for height." - what kind of sentence is this, I don't see any y part on the graphic!”

  • You are right: the picture drew only the x line. The sentence pointed at two lines that were not drawn.
  • It meant: do the x line's drawing two more times, with two other lines through S.
    • The y line (orange) goes along y, toward the bottom of the picture. The line from the tip of N meets it 0.402 from S: N's y part.
    • The height line (blue) goes straight up. The line from the tip of N meets it 0.864 from S: N's height part.
  • Each line from the tip of N meets its line at a square corner.
  • This picture shows all three at once.
  • Step 2 now has the buttons x line, y line and height line, which show them one at a time.

3 · Where F is when L points the same way as N

“"Same direction as N: F is the tip of N, so the number is 1." - what you mean by "F is the tip of N", isn't just F staying exactly below N instead?! What's up with those made up words?”

  • F is not “below N”. Step 3 says where F is: where the line from the tip of N meets L's line at a square corner.
  • The tip of N is the end of N with the arrowhead.
  • Frame 1: Step 3's flat picture draws L's line left to right. So the line from the tip of N goes straight down the picture, and F looks “below N”.
  • Frames 2 and 3: L turns toward N, and L's line turns with it. The line from the tip of N stays at a square corner to L's line, so it tilts and gets shorter.
  • Frame 4: L points the same way as N, so L's line runs through the tip of N. The line from the tip has length 0, so F is at the tip of N, 1 from S: the number is 1.
  • “F is the tip of N” should have said “F is at the tip of N”. Step 4 now says so.

4 · −1: F on the minus side

“"Opposite to N: F lies 1 behind P, so the number is −1." - what's meant by that?!?!?!?!??!!?!?!?”

  • “Behind P” was a made-up phrase. It meant: on the minus side of the ruler on L's line.
  • The ruler: 0 at S, plus numbers on the side L points to, minus numbers on the other side.
  • Left: the lesson's L. The tip of N is on the side L points to, so the ruler reads plus at F: about 0.46.
  • Right: L opposite to N. L's line again runs through the tip of N, so F is at the tip of N, as in card 3.
  • But L now points away from the tip of N, so the tip of N is on the minus side. It is 1 from S, so the ruler reads −1 at F.
  • Step 4 now says: “Opposite to N: F is at the tip of N again, now 1 on the minus side: the number is −1.”

5 · The minus side, on the picture

“"Minus means: F lies behind P, on the side L does not point to." - and this, is this on the graphic?!???!”

  • No, it was not. Step 4's ruler had tick marks, but no numbers and no names for its two sides.
  • Now Step 4's picture prints the ruler's numbers and names the plus side and the minus side.
  • Here L is turned so that F lies 0.5 from S on the minus side. The ruler reads −0.5 at F, so dot(N, L) = −0.5.
  • A number below 0 means: N points more away from L than toward it.

6 · Step 6's triangles: the third fact

“In step 6 of section 1 you just briefly mention that triangles have equal sides and angle of 90 deg, this is not enough for congruent triangles, they need one more rule. Who are you trying to fool?”

  • You are right. A square corner and a long side of 1 do not fix a triangle.
  • Left: from X, a line at a square corner to L's line meets it at J. A line at a square corner to another line through S meets that line at R.
  • S, X, J and S, X, R both have the long side from S to X, 1, and a square corner. But they differ: their other sides are 0.48 and 0.877, then 0.8 and 0.6.
  • The missing fact is the corner at S. Right: S, X, J and S, the tip of L, Q both lie between the x line and L's line, so they have the same corner at S.
  • With all three facts, only one triangle fits:
    • put the long side 1 along one of the two lines;
    • the other side must run along the other line, because the corner at S is fixed;
    • the square corner fixes where that side ends.
  • Step 6's flip does exactly this: it swaps the x line and L's line, so it keeps the corner at S and takes X onto the tip of L.
  • Step 7 uses the same fact. Grow the small lime triangle from S until its long side is 1: its square corner and its corner at S stay, so it lands on the big one. So the two have the same shape.
  • Step 6 now names all three facts and marks the corner at S with an arc. Step 7 says why its two triangles have the same shape.
