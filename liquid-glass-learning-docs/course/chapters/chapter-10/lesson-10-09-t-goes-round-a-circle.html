Lesson 10.9 — T goes round a circle through the tip of N

Spin the teal line a full turn: its end T draws a circle on the wall, and the tip of N is on it. Light theme · desktop · works from file://.

The spin keeps T on the wall (Lesson 10.6) and 0.953 from Gx (Lesson 10.8). This lesson puts the two facts together: T's path is a circle on the wall around Gx, and the tip of N is one of its points, so a spin of some angle brings T there. Lesson 10.16 uses the teal line in that place, lying along the line from Gx to the tip of N.

Exercise 1 — Which of the points a, b and c on the wall can T reach at some spin?

Why this question. All three points are on the wall, like T. Being on the wall is not enough for T to get there.

What you do. For each point, work out how far it is from Gx: its y part and its height part meet at a square corner (Lesson 10.7, Step 4), so Lesson 9.1 gives its length. Compare with Gx to T.

Question: which of the points a, b and c on the wall can T reach at some spin?

Show the answer
  • T's circle: every point of the wall 0.953 from Gx.
  • Point a, from Gx:
    • y part² = (−0.5) × (−0.5) = 0.25
    • height part² = 0.5 × 0.5 = 0.25
    • length² = 0.25 + 0.25 = 0.5
    • length = √0.5 = 0.707
    • Inside the circle.
  • Point b, from Gx:
    • y part² = 0.864 × 0.864 = 0.746496
    • height part² = 0.402 × 0.402 = 0.161604
    • length² = 0.746496 + 0.161604 = 0.9081
    • length = √0.9081 = 0.953
    • On the circle.
  • Point c, from Gx:
    • y part² = (−0.8) × (−0.8) = 0.64
    • height part² = 0.8 × 0.8 = 0.64
    • length² = 0.64 + 0.64 = 1.28
    • length = √1.28 = 1.131
    • Outside the circle.
  • So only point b is on T's circle: in a full turn T passes it, so a spin of some angle brings T there.
Exercise 2 — Point d is 0.953 from Gx. Can a spin bring T to point d?

Why this question. Point d is exactly as far from Gx as T. Is that enough for T to get there?

What you do. Check both things a point of T's circle needs (Step 2): on the wall, and 0.953 from Gx.

Question: point d is 0.953 from Gx. Can a spin bring T to point d?

Show the answer
  • But its x part is 0.402: it is 0.302 + 0.402 = 0.704 to the right of S, off the wall.
  • T's circle lies on the wall (a line in the “Wall edge-on” view). So no spin brings T to point d.
