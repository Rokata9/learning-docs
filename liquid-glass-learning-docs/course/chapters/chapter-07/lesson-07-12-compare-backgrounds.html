Chapter 7 · Same tree, five kinds of pixels behind it

Compare backgrounds

Question: Lesson 7.11 froze one production tree: shadow outside the clip, then fill, grain, band, rim, and a sharp label. That tree still sits on the navy-and-orange study picture. Does the same recipe keep a white label readable, and keep the destination quiet, when the pixels behind the glass are a photo-like field, sharp text, a slow gradient, a dark field, or a light field?

A destination class is a name for the kind of pixels the glass is drawn over. The new measurement is: run the frozen 7.11 recipe, sample one interior RGB, compute the Lesson 3.7 contrast of the white label against that RGB, and write pass if the ratio is at least 4.5:1, else fail.
This lesson does not rebuild slot assignment, blur, tint, saturation, or the contrast formula. Lesson 3.6 already defined relative luminance. Lesson 3.7 already defined the contrast ratio and the 4.5:1 normal-text target. The fill draws an owned copy of the destination class. Chapter 6 already taught live backdrop sampling. This lesson does not rebuild capture.

1 · THE NEW OBJECTS

A class of destination, then a verdict

Destination class

One of five kinds of source pixels: photo-like colored patches, text as repeated sharp glyphs, a gradient that changes slowly from left to right, a uniform dark field, or a uniform light field. The surround and the owned fill copy use the same class.

Interior sample

One pixel on the panel’s vertical center line, above the label, after the frozen recipe and before the label is drawn. Its stored straight RGB is the background for the contrast measurement. The yellow cross marks that pixel.

Verdict

Pass means white-label contrast ≥ 4.5:1. Fail means it is below that target. The stronger of black and white is still reported. A fail does not tell you to raise blur radius. Lesson 3.7 already treated the target as a separate check from “which candidate is stronger.”

interior RGB = frozen 7.11 recipe at the sample above the label white contrast = Lesson 3.7 ratio of white against that RGB verdict = pass if white contrast ≥ 4.5, else fail
Structure leftover is an observation by eye, not a 4.5 pass or fail. After the frozen recipe, look at the frosted fill. Can you still name the destination class from that fill?

A uniform field is a destination where every pixel starts as one color. The dark class is rgb(26, 64, 102) at every pixel. The light class is rgb(224, 230, 237) at every pixel. There are no letters and no photo blobs. After frost you still name a color neighborhood: the interior is still a dark navy, or still a light gray. That remaining neighborhood is the leftover on a uniform field. The old phrase “color family” meant this neighborhood. It did not mean a new formula. Canonical wording: structure leftover, local backing.

They stay sharp because they are not in the 18 CSS px fill blur. Leftover is only destination content still recognizable inside the frosted fill. A blurry fill is the recipe working. It is not a leftover “pass.”

Both miss means white contrast is below 4.5 and black contrast is below 4.5. Then neither label color meets the target. The live numbers above tell you whether that happened on this class.

A material change is a change that moves the interior RGB: raise the Lesson 7.5 tint amount above 0.25, or add a local backing. It is not a larger blur radius. Lesson 7.6 already said saturation is not an accessibility control.

Local backing is not the 7.9 shadowa plate under the letters, not a halo around the card

Use a backing only if both label colors miss 4.5. On this lesson’s five classes, one of the two candidates always passes, so the live readout should say both-miss is false. The backing picture is the later repair, not a hidden part of the current tree.

4 · THE PRODUCTION RULE

After you have seen a fail on light

keep the 7.11 tree frozen
sample the interior after the recipe
measure white-label contrast
pass at 4.5:1, otherwise pick the stronger candidate or change a material input

Inputs

Destination class — the five buttons. It is the only controlled input.

Frozen recipe — Lesson 7.11 slots plus 7.6–7.10 constants.

White label — straight sRGB (1, 1, 1) with alpha 1. Relative luminance of that white is 1.

Outputs, and who consumes them

Interior RGB is consumed by relative luminance, then by the contrast ratio.

White contrast is consumed by the 4.5 verdict.

Structure leftover is consumed by eye, not by the ratio. Readable glyphs through the fill are leftover even when contrast passes. The sharp rim is not leftover.

6 · CHAPTER 7 CHECKPOINT

A polished frost is still not Liquid Glass

The production card can look finished on a dark field. It is still a frosted material without a lens. These absences are the chapter boundary, not defects in the 7.11 tree.

No refraction

Straight lines behind the card do not bend at the path. Chapter 9 will displace the sample coordinate.

No custom shader

The fill, grain, band, and shadow are SwiftUI views and bitmaps. Chapter 8 will run one small function per output pixel.

Rim and band are not lighting

One color still runs around every side. A later lighting chapter will make the bright side depend on a lamp direction.

Shadow is not a lamp

The downward offset remains a convention from Lesson 7.9. No sun is simulated.

No morph

The rounded rectangle does not squash on press or merge with a neighbor. Chapter 5 taught motion. Chapter 12 will reconnect it to this material.

Owned copy, not live capture

The fill draws a destination-class copy. Chapter 6 already taught recording the real backdrop. Production would use that path. This chapter did not rebuild it.

10 · EXERCISES

Answer before opening each hidden solution

What is a destination class in this lesson?

A name for the kind of pixels the glass is drawn over: photo-like patches, text glyphs, a slow gradient, a dark field, or a light field. The surround and the owned fill copy share that class.

Diagnostic: white contrast is 3.1:1 and black contrast is 6.8:1 on the light class. Did the recipe break?

No. The frozen recipe still ran. White failed the 4.5 target because the interior RGB is light. Black is the stronger candidate and passes. Both-miss is false, because black is above 4.5. Changing blur radius is not the repair. Choose black. A local backing is a later plate under the letters, not the 7.9 shadow, and it is not needed while one candidate still passes.

The text class still shows destination letters in the frosted fill. The rim is sharp. Did leftover “pass”?

Leftover is not a 4.5 pass. Destination letters still readable in the fill means leftover is present. The sharp rim is a later sibling, not leftover. A blurry fill is the 18 CSS px blur working. Contrast is a separate measurement of the label against the interior RGB, and it can pass or fail independently of leftover.

Name three reasons this polished card is still not Liquid Glass.

Any three from: no refraction, no custom per-pixel shader, rim and band are not lighting, the shadow offset is not a lamp, no press morph, and the fill is an owned copy rather than a live backdrop sample.

12 · EVIDENCE BOUNDARY

What this lesson establishes

Established

The same 7.11 tree yields different white-label verdicts on different destination classes. Light interiors can fail 4.5 for white while black passes. Structure leftover is an eye observation of remaining destination structure in the fill, not a 4.5 pass. A uniform field’s leftover is a remaining color neighborhood. A local backing is a plate under the letters, not the 7.9 shadow. Chapter 7’s frost is still not a lens, a lamp, a shader, or a morph.

Not claimed

The photo-like patches are not a camera photograph. The owned fill copy is not live backdrop capture. The explorer’s luminance is the Lesson 3.6 teaching decode, the same arithmetic this page shows. Chapter 8 begins per-pixel GPU programs.
