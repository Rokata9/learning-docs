Saturation and contrast

Question: After blur and a pale tint, orange can still shout. How do we keep navy and orange present, but quiet enough that a sharp label can be read?

A saturation factor below 1 moves every glass pixel closer to its own gray reference. Contrast is then measured from that new RGB, to choose white or black for the label.
Lesson 3.9 already taught the factor. Lesson 3.7 already taught the contrast ratio. This lesson attaches those two finished operations to the frosted stack. It does not rebuild the 4×5 matrix rows.

1 · TWO DIFFERENT JOBS

Saturation transforms. Contrast measures.

Saturation — a transform

Lesson 3.9: one factor scales each channel’s signed distance from that lesson’s gray reference. Factor 1 leaves the tinted interiors unchanged. Factor 0.40 moves them closer to gray. Factor 0 makes each interior a gray whose three channels equal that interior’s own gray reference.

Contrast — a measurement

Lesson 3.7: after the transform, relative luminance of the new RGB is calculated, then

contrast ratio = (lighter luminance + 0.05) ÷ (darker luminance + 0.05)

That number compares a label color with the glass pixel behind it. It is not a second matrix.

Do not mix the gray reference with relative luminance. Saturation uses stored-channel weights 0.213, 0.715, 0.072. Contrast uses decoded linear-light luminance from Lesson 3.6. Same-looking coefficients, different jobs.

6 · iOS ROUTE

Filter the glass group. Keep the label outside.

var saturationMatrix = LabColorMatrix()
saturationMatrix.setToSaturation(saturationFactor)

ZStack {
    Canvas { context, size in
        drawLessonArtwork(context: &context, size: size)
    }
    .blur(radius: 18, opaque: false)

    Rectangle()
        .fill(whiteTint.opacity(0.25))
}
.compositingGroup()
.colorEffect(
    ShaderLibrary.saturationOnGlassGroup(
        .float(Float(saturationFactor)),
        .float(0.213),
        .float(0.715),
        .float(0.072)
    )
)

Text("Sharp label")

What each part changes

The inner canvas is the Lesson 7.4/7.5 artwork. .blur(radius: 18, opaque: false) blurs that view. 18 is points. displayScale is not a blur input.

The rectangle is the Lesson 7.5 white veil with amount fixed at 0.25.

compositingGroup flattens the blur and the tint into one layer. colorEffect then runs saturationOnGlassGroup on that layer. The weights are 0.213, 0.715, and 0.072. setToSaturation writes the same weights into the 4×5 matrix used for the interior readout. That attachment is the new step. It is not Core Image’s saturation control.

The label is a later sibling, not a child of the filtered group, so it is not desaturated or blurred.

9 · EXPLAIN IT BACK

Four checks

What new attachment does this lesson add?

A colorEffect named saturationOnGlassGroup, using the same weights as setToSaturation, on the already blurred and tinted glass group. The label stays outside that group.

Why does factor 0 not make navy and orange identical?

Each pixel has its own gray reference from its own RGB. Navy’s gray is darker than orange’s gray. Factor 0 removes chroma but can leave a lightness difference.

Why recalculate contrast after changing the factor?

Contrast is a measurement of the current RGB. After saturation, the RGB is different, so an old contrast number is stale.

Why is the label outside the filtered group?

If the label were inside the colorEffect group, its own RGB would also move toward gray. Lesson 6.11 already kept labels outside effect layers so they stay sharp and fully colored.

11 · EVIDENCE BOUNDARY

What this lesson establishes

Established

After blur and a pale tint, setToSaturation can quiet chroma on the glass group. The explorer attaches that transform with saturationOnGlassGroup. Foreground contrast must be measured from the transformed RGB. The label stays outside the filter.

Not claimed

Saturation does not guarantee a 4.5 : 1 pass. The lesson does not rebuild the 4×5 matrix derivation, does not treat the gray reference as luminance, and does not claim Apple’s private glass recipe.
