The lab code does the same two photocopies: draw the photo onto the scratch paper (shrink), then draw the scratch paper onto the display (enlarge). Blur happens on the scratch paper, between the two photocopies. This page ignores blur on purpose. We are only answering: which part of the photo is stored, and where does it land?

1 · THE ONLY RULE

A thumbtack stays put. Everything else slides.

A pivot is a thumbtack pushed through the paper. That one point does not move. Every other point moves toward the thumbtack when you shrink, or away from it when you enlarge.

In words: measure how far a point is from the thumbtack, multiply that distance by the scale, then walk the new distance starting from the thumbtack.

new position = thumbtack position + (old position − thumbtack position) × scale scale 0.5 = half as far from the thumbtack as before (shrink) scale 2 = twice as far from the thumbtack as before (enlarge)

2 · THE TRAP

The lesson does not shrink inside the photo’s paper.

Your familiar picture keeps the 8 × 8 destination and shrinks the artwork inside it. This lesson’s route first creates a new 4 × 4 paper, then draws onto that new paper. The thumbtack is chosen on the paper you are drawing onto, not on the original photo.

Familiar · same 8 × 8 paper

The destination stays 8 × 8. A “center” thumbtack is the photo’s center (4, 4). Empty margins appear around a smaller picture.

This lesson · new 4 × 4 paper

The destination is only 4 × 4. A “center” thumbtack is the scratch paper’s center (2, 2). That is a different point.

Line the top-left corners up. Then look at the number line.

The code draws the photo into the scratch paper with both local top-left corners at (0, 0). No extra shift. So the number 2 means two units from the shared top-left — on both papers.

This is the whole trick. The scratch paper’s center sits at 2. The photo’s center sits at 4. After the top-lefts are lined up, a “center” thumbtack is only one quarter of the way across the photo. Shrinking toward that thumbtack keeps the photo’s top-left and throws the photo’s right side and bottom off the small paper.

The first scale is 0.5. It happens while recording into the 4 × 4 paper. Anything that lands outside 0…4 is not stored. Those original pixels are gone. The later enlargement cannot invent them back.

6 · BACK TO THE LESSON NUMBERS

Same geometry. Just more pixels.

The lesson uses a portrait near the Preview size, not 8 × 8. The two photocopies and the two thumbtacks are unchanged.

Toy model on this page

photo = 8 × 8 scratch paper = 8 × 0.5 = 4 × 4 enlarge scale = 1 ÷ 0.5 = 2 scratch center = (2, 2) photo center = (4, 4)

Lesson 7.4 production numbers

photo = 483 × 528 scratch width = round(483 × 0.5) = 242 scratch height = round(528 × 0.5) = 264 enlarge scale = 2 scratch center ≈ (121, 132) photo center = (241.5, 264)

The extra 0.5 pixel of width (241.5 rounded up to 242) only adds a sliver of empty or overflow. It is not why a case looks shifted or cropped. The shift and the crop come from which thumbtack you used, on which paper.

Production choice in the iOS explorer: both thumbtacks stay at the top-left (0, 0). That is Case 1. The whole photo lands on the scratch paper. The scratch paper then fills the display. No discarded region. No empty margin. The other three cases exist so you can see what those two thumbtacks actually do. You do not want Cases 2–4 in the frosted-glass path.

The two Swift sites

recordingPivot = .zero // first photocopy: shrink onto the scratch bitmap placementPivot = .zero enlargeScale = 1 / workingScale // second photocopy: enlarge onto the display

.zero means the thumbtack is the top-left of the scratch bitmap both times. Changing only the placement pivot moves the already-blurred image. Changing the recording pivot changes which original pixels were ever stored.

7 · CHECK YOURSELF

Do not open the hidden answers first. Use the 8 × 8 model.

1. During the first photocopy, the thumbtack is at the scratch paper’s center (2, 2). Does the green dot at (6.5, 2.2) get stored? Why?

2. The scratch paper already holds the whole photo (Case 1’s first photocopy). You then enlarge around the scratch paper’s center. Which part of the original photo is still visible on the 8 × 8 display, and why is the top-left of the display not the top-left of the photo?

3. Someone says: “If I change the placement pivot to the center, I will crop a different part of the original photo.” What is wrong with that sentence?

Show answers

1. No. New x = 2 + (6.5 − 2) × 0.5 = 4.25. The scratch paper only exists from 0 to 4. The green dot lands just past the right edge, so it is discarded.

2. Visible original region is (2, 2)…(8, 8): the bottom-right 6 × 6 of the photo. Enlarging around (2, 2) sends the stored top-left (0, 0) to (−2, −2), which is off the display. This is not “one stored pixel becoming a 2 × 2 block.” It is the same thumbtack rule run backwards, once per axis, to ask which stored point landed on the display’s top-left.

Forward enlarge: new = thumbtack + (old − thumbtack) × 2. Inverse: old = thumbtack + (new − thumbtack) ÷ 2. Display top-left is (0, 0), enlarge thumbtack is (2, 2), so scratch x = 2 + (0 − 2) ÷ 2 = 1, and scratch y is the same. Display (0, 0) therefore shows the color that was stored at scratch (1, 1). That stored point itself came from the first photocopy with shrink thumbtack (0, 0) and scale 0.5: original = 0 + (1 − 0) ÷ 0.5 = 2 on both axes. So display (0, 0) is original (2, 2).

3. The placement pivot runs after the scratch bitmap is already filled. It only moves those stored pixels on the display. The original region was chosen by the recording scale plus the 4 × 4 bounds. A later thumbtack cannot put discarded original pixels back.
