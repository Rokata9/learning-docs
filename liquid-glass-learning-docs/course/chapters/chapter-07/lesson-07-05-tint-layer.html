Chapter 7 · A pale material color after blur

Tint layer

Question: After we already have a blurred backdrop, how do we add a pale glass color without turning navy and orange into the same slab?

A tint layer is a second draw: one chosen color with a chosen alpha, painted over the already-blurred image.
Lesson 3.8 already taught the mix. This lesson does not invent a new color formula. It places that mix after blur, as its own draw, so the wash can change without running the blur again.

1 · THE NEW OBJECT

Blur finished. The destination is now an image.

1 · Blurred destination

The image produced by Lessons 7.1–7.4. Different positions still have different colors. Navy interior and orange interior are still two destinations.

2 · Tint source

One straight sRGB color plus an alpha. The alpha is the tint amount. White with amount 0.25 is a pale veil.

3 · Output

Source-over of that tint over the blurred image. Navy stays in the navy family. Orange stays in the orange family. Both pick up the same wash.

This is not a blur setting. Changing the tint amount does not change which neighbors were averaged. The destination colors already exist.

4 · ONE CHANNEL, THEN TWO POSITIONS

The 3.8 short formula, now over a blurred interior

Lesson 3.8 already reduced source-over to this short form when the destination is opaque. The blurred interiors in this lesson are treated as opaque.

output = tint × amount + destination × (1 − amount) = destination + amount × (tint − destination)

Teaching interiors

navy destination = (0.10, 0.25, 0.40) orange destination = (0.90, 0.60, 0.20) white tint = (1.00, 1.00, 1.00) amount = 0.25

These interiors sit far from the navy/orange join, so blur does not mix the two families. The exact neighborhood average was Lesson 7.1. Here the destination of the tint draw is this known interior color.

5 · THREE AMOUNTS

Pale keeps identity. Amount 1 deletes it.

Background identity means a viewer can still tell the navy side from the orange side. At amount 1.00 both interiors become the tint color, so identity is gone. Frosted glass stays in the pale range.
6 · WHY A SEPARATE DRAW AFTER BLUR

Independence, not a different mix

If the tint lived inside the blur source

Changing the wash would require running the blur again. Labels drawn into that same source would also be blurred.

Production order in this lesson

blurred layer → drawRect of tint × amount → sharp label on top

The wash is a cheap later draw. Lesson 6.11 already kept labels outside the effect layer. They stay outside the tint layer too.

Uniform tint and a linear blur commute. Tinting the whole image and then blurring it produces the same pixels as blurring first and then applying the same uniform tint. We still draw tint after blur so the wash and the labels stay independent of the blur pass. Do not claim the colors would look different solely because of that order.
7 · ANDROID ROUTE

One drawRect after the blurred layer

val tintSource = tintColor.copy(alpha = tintAmount)

Canvas(Modifier.matchParentSize().graphicsLayer {
    renderEffect = BlurEffect(
        renderEffect = null,
        radiusX = radiusPx,
        radiusY = radiusPx,
        edgeTreatment = TileMode.Clamp,
    )
}) {
    drawArtwork()
}

Canvas(Modifier.matchParentSize()) {
    drawRect(color = tintSource)
}

What each part changes

The first Canvas owns only the artwork and the blur. Its output is the destination image.

The second Canvas draws one rectangle of the tint source. That is the tint layer.

tintColor.copy(alpha = tintAmount) is the Lesson 3.8 source: chosen RGB, amount as alpha.

TileMode.Clamp stays fixed. Edge treatment is not the new mechanism.

10 · EXPLAIN IT BACK

Four checks

What new object does this lesson add?

A tint layer: a second draw of one color with a chosen alpha, over the already-blurred image.

Why can navy and orange still look different at amount 0.25?

Each position keeps 75% of its own destination color. Navy and orange destinations are still different, so the outputs stay in different families.

Why is the tint not a blur setting?

Blur already chose the destination colors by averaging neighbors. The tint draw only mixes those finished colors with one source color. Changing the amount does not change which neighbors were read.

Why draw the tint after the blur instead of mixing it into the blur source?

Then the wash and the sharp labels can change without running the blur again. A uniform tint and a linear blur would produce the same pixels either way; the later draw is for independence, not for a different mix.

12 · EVIDENCE BOUNDARY

What this lesson establishes

Established

After a blurred destination exists, a later drawRect of a translucent tint source performs the Lesson 3.8 mix at every panel position. A pale amount keeps two different interiors distinguishable.

Not claimed

This is not Apple's private glass tint, not a ColorFilter contract, and not a claim that blur-then-tint produces different pixels than a uniform tint-then-linear-blur. Saturation and contrast stay in Lesson 7.6.
