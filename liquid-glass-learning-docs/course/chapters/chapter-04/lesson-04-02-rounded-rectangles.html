Lesson 4.2 — Rounded rectangles

Lesson 4.1 gave you a rectangle whose left, top, right, and bottom boundaries are already known. This lesson keeps those boundaries fixed. It changes one new input: the corner radius.

Practical question

When a sharp rectangle corner becomes curved, where does each straight edge stop, where is the curve centered, and which parts of the rectangle stay unchanged?

One new geometry input

Corner radius controls the size of all four curved corners in this controlled lesson.

The rectangle bounds do not move. Only the boundary near each corner changes.

1 · WHERE THIS LESSON FITS

Keep the Lesson 4.1 rectangle and replace its sharp corners

No second shape operation, blur, or animation is added.

Known bounds

You already know left, top, right, bottom, width, and height.

Known circle radius

You already know that a radius is a center-to-boundary distance.

New input

One corner radius is used by all four corners.

New output

A rounded rectangle with the same outer bounds and four curved corners.

Deliberate boundary: this lesson uses the same horizontal and vertical radius, so every corner curve is a quarter of a circle. Later lessons cover different horizontal and vertical radii, different values at individual corners, and boundaries built from connected drawing segments.
2 · WHAT YOU WILL SEE

The bounds stay fixed while the corner regions change

These three shapes use the same rectangle bounds.

What stays fixed: the leftmost, topmost, rightmost, and bottommost positions remain the same in all three cards. The corner radius changes only how the boundary travels between adjacent straight edges.
3 · TERMS

Define the corner objects before using formulas

Every term points to a visible object in the diagram.

Rounded rectangle

A rectangle whose sharp corners have been replaced by curved boundary pieces.

Corner radius

The distance from one corner curve's center to that curve.

Arc

One connected piece of a circle's boundary.

Quarter-circle

One of four equal arcs made by dividing a circle into four parts.

Join point

The exact position where a straight edge ends and the corner arc begins.

Construction circle

A full dashed circle shown only to explain the quarter-circle used by the final corner.

9 · EXPLAIN THE RESULT

Radius changes the route between two fixed edges

What stays fixed

Left, top, right, bottom, width, and height remain unchanged. The rounded rectangle still touches the same four extreme boundary positions.

What changes

Each sharp corner is replaced by one quarter-circle arc. The two join points move farther from the sharp corner as the radius grows.

Why two radii are subtracted

The top edge loses one radius at the left corner and one radius at the right corner. The same two-end rule applies to each side edge.

Maximum used here: the radius is limited to half the shorter side. At radius 130, the left and right straight segments become zero because 260 − 2 × 130 = 0. Lesson 4.3 gives that special boundary shape its own name and contract.

12 · HIDDEN ANSWERS

Open only after answering in chat or on paper

Answer 1
center X = left + radius = 120 + 50 = 170 center Y = top + radius = 80 + 50 = 130 curve center = (170, 130)
Answer 2
top join = (left + radius, top) = (170, 80) left join = (left, top + radius) = (120, 130)
Answer 3

The four outer bounds do not change. The curve centers and join points move farther inward. Each top or bottom join moves by the radius change along X. Each left or right join moves by the radius change along Y. The straight edge spans become shorter.

Answer 4
maximum radius = height ÷ 2 = 260 ÷ 2 = 130 left-side straight length = height − 2 × radius = 260 − 260 = 0
