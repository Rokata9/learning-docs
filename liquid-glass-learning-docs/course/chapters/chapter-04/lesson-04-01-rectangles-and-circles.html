Lesson 4.1 — Rectangles and circles

Lesson 2.4 already gave you a rectangle with left, top, right, bottom, width, height, and center. This lesson keeps that rectangle and adds one circle. The practical task is to identify the exact numbers iOS needs before it can draw either shape.

In this lesson, geometry means the numbers that locate and size a shape. A boundary is the line separating positions inside the shape from positions outside it.

Practical question

How do the geometry inputs for a rectangle differ from the geometry inputs for a circle, and how does each input determine the visible boundary?

One new distinction

A rectangle has four straight boundary coordinates. This lesson supplies left, top, width, and height, then derives right and bottom. A circle uses a center and one radius, meaning the straight distance from the center to the circle boundary. This lesson measures that distance horizontally.

The circle also has an enclosing square: the smallest rectangle with horizontal and vertical edges that contains the complete circle. That square helps us report the circle's extreme positions, but the square is not the circle.

3 · TERMS

Name every object before the first formula

Shape

A filled or outlined area selected by a boundary.

Boundary

The line that separates positions inside a shape from positions outside it.

Radius

One straight-line distance from a circle's center to its boundary. This lesson measures that distance horizontally.

Diameter

The full distance across a circle through its center. It contains two radii.

Rectangle bounds

The left, top, right, and bottom edge coordinates that enclose the rectangle. The browser derives right and bottom from left, top, width, and height. SwiftUI Canvas receives the equivalent origin plus size.

Circle's enclosing square

The smallest rectangle with horizontal and vertical edges that contains the complete circle. Its four corner regions are outside the circle.

6 · iOS CONNECTION

Request layout in points; SwiftUI Canvas already draws in points

Complete Swift files remain in Xcode and LiquidGlassLab-iOS.

A SwiftUI Canvas gives its drawing closure a GraphicsContext and a CGSize. The size and every drawing coordinate inside the closure are in points. Lesson 2.5 already taught px = pt × displayScale for physical pixels and shader pixel math.

1 · Layout request

The explorer requests a controlled 320 × 220 point drawing surface.

2 · Canvas coordinates

SwiftUI Canvas drawing is already in points. There is no density conversion on the draw path.

3 · Rectangle inputs

Path(CGRect) receives a point-space origin and a point-space size.

4 · Circle inputs

Path(ellipseIn:) receives a point-space center and one radius in points.

5 · Pixels

The measurement card reports physical pixels as points × displayScale. SwiftUI later produces the visible pixels.

Short rectangle excerpt

// RectanglesCirclesExplorer.swift — ShapeCanvas
let rectangle = CGRect(
    x: rectangleLeft,
    y: rectangleTop,
    width: rectangleWidth,
    height: rectangleHeight
)
context.fill(Path(rectangle), with: .color(rectangleColor.opacity(0.34)))
context.stroke(Path(rectangle), with: .color(rectangleColor), lineWidth: 3)

Short circle excerpt

// RectanglesCirclesExplorer.swift — ShapeCanvas
let circleCenter = CGPoint(x: circleCenterX, y: circleCenterY)
let circleRect = CGRect(
    x: circleCenter.x - circleRadius,
    y: circleCenter.y - circleRadius,
    width: circleRadius * 2,
    height: circleRadius * 2
)
context.fill(Path(ellipseIn: circleRect), with: .color(circleColor.opacity(0.32)))
context.stroke(Path(ellipseIn: circleRect), with: .color(circleColor), lineWidth: 3)
Evidence boundary: the Swift code proves which geometry values are sent to SwiftUI Canvas. The iOS screen reports the measured Canvas size in points and the physical-pixel size as points × displayScale. This lesson does not read rendered pixels back from the graphics processing unit, and it does not yet explain the softened boundary pixels created by antialiasing.

9 · EXPLAIN THE RESULT

The two draw calls consume different geometry objects

QuestionRectangleCircle
What locates it?Left and top, or an equivalent top-left point.Center X and center Y.
What sizes it?Width and height can change independently.One radius changes horizontal and vertical extent together.
What are its extreme bounds?The four rectangle edges are the shape boundary.Center ± radius gives the leftmost, rightmost, topmost, and bottommost positions.
Are its bounds the same as its filled area?Yes, for this plain rectangle.No. The enclosing square contains four corner areas outside the circle.
Likely misconception: a circle's enclosing square does not turn the circle into a square. The square is a separate rectangle used only to report the circle's outer horizontal and vertical coordinates. Later clipping and mask lessons will rely on this object distinction.

Exercise 3 · Diagnostic: is the enclosing square part of the circle?

The circle's enclosing square is 150 × 150. Does every position inside that square belong to the circle?

Show answer

No. The diagram shows the curved circle boundary leaving the square's four corner regions unfilled. The square reports the circle's extreme horizontal and vertical bounds; it is not the circular filled area. Calculating the distance to an arbitrary corner point is deferred until Chapter 9.

Exercise 4 · Move the center

Show answer

Leftmost X changes from 445 to 465. Rightmost X changes from 595 to 615. Center Y, topmost, bottommost, radius, and diameter stay unchanged.
