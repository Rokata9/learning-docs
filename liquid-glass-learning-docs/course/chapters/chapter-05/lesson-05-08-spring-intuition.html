3 · SIGN CYCLE

Crossings and turning points change different signs

Target crossing

Position moves from one side of the target to the other.

d = T − x changes sign S = k × d changes sign v may still keep moving in the previous direction

Peak or trough

The motion turns around.

v changes sign D = −c × v changes sign x begins moving in the opposite direction
Durable model: crossings flip displacement and spring pull. Turning points flip velocity. The time gap between those events is what allows overshoot.

7 · EXERCISES

Answer before opening the solutions

1 · At a target crossing, which values must change sign?

d = T − x changes sign because x moves to the other side of T. S = k × d changes sign with d because k is positive. Velocity does not have to change sign at the crossing.

2 · At a peak above the target, what is true about velocity and spring pull?

Velocity is zero at the turning point and then becomes negative. Displacement is negative, so spring pull is also negative and points back toward the target.

3 · Diagnostic: why is position error alone insufficient for settling?

A value can pass close to the target while still moving quickly. The settle diagnostic therefore checks both position error and speed magnitude.

4 · Why are d and S shaped similarly in the graph?

S = k × d. For one fixed positive k, spring pull is a scaled copy of displacement. Their zero crossings and signs match.
