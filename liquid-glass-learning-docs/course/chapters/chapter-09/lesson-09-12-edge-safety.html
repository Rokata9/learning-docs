Lesson 9.12 — Edge safety

What the glass shows when it reads past the edge of the wallpaper. Light theme · desktop · works from file://.

In Lesson 9.11 every pixel took its color from a point of the wallpaper, its read point, and the glass sat in the middle of the screen. Now the glass moves to the bottom edge of the screen. Some read points then land below the bottom edge of the wallpaper, where there is no wallpaper. Those pixels become see-through. One line of code fixes this: clamp.

  • The wallpaper: the orange and navy checkerboard from Lesson 9.7. The shader reads its colors.
  • Hatch: no color at all. The pixel is transparent (see-through).
  • The white circle: the glass from Lesson 9.11, radius 0.25. Its center C = (0.50, 1.10) is 0.10 below the screen.
  • The yellow line: the bottom edge of the wallpaper, y = 1.00.

Section 2 · the fixClamp the read point back onto the wallpaper

The fix is one new line, right after the line that computes the read point:

readUv = uv − disp the read point R (Lesson 9.11) readUv = clamp(readUv, 0.0, 1.0) new: move R back onto the wallpaper → R′

clamp(x, lo, hi) keeps one number inside the range from lo to hi. A number below lo becomes lo. A number above hi becomes hi. A number between them does not change. Drag the dot x along the line:

A read point has two numbers, x and y. clamp(readUv, 0.0, 1.0) clamps each number separately: x into 0 … 1, and y into 0 … 1. So a read point outside the wallpaper jumps to the nearest point of the wallpaper's edge, and a read point inside does not move. We clamp because every point of the edge has a color: after the clamp, every pixel gets a color, and no pixel is see-through. Drag R:

  • the wallpaper, from 0 to 1 in x and in y
  • the yellow line: the edge of the wallpaper, on all four sides
  • R: a read point (drag it)
  • R′: the clamped read point
  • the lime clamp arrow: from R to R′

What clamp costs. Clamp does not invent the missing wallpaper. The pixels of the seam all read the bottom edge, so the bottom row of the wallpaper repeats downward. On the checkerboard you cannot see this, because the bottom row of cells keeps the same color all the way down. On the screen wallpaper you can. The screen is a settings list, drawn by this page; its last row, “Privacy & Security”, is cut in half by the bottom edge. With clamp, the letters of that row turn into short vertical streaks inside the seam (see the magnifier). Streaks still give every pixel a real color; the seam gives no color at all.

Another fix: pad. Lesson 7.3 fixed the same problem for blur in a different way: read a bigger rectangle of the real picture behind the glass, then keep only the middle. That fix is called pad. Pad needs real pixels beyond the edge. Below the bottom edge of the screen there are no pixels, so here clamp is the fix.

Why pixels 1 to 6 are turned around. Pixels 1 to 6 are closer than 0.25 − 0.10 = 0.15 to C. There sd = d − 0.25 is below −0.10, so smoothstep = 0 and falloff = 1 − 0 = 1 (the chain). So each of them gets the full push: push = 0.20 × 1 = 0.20. Each is also closer than 0.20 to C. So its pink arrow passes C and ends on the other side of C (Lesson 9.11). Pixel 2 as an example: pixel 2 = (0.56, 0.50) is 0.56 − 0.50 = 0.06 to the right of C. The push moves the read point 0.20 to the left: 0.56 − 0.20 = 0.36. The read point (0.36, 0.50) is 0.50 − 0.36 = 0.14 to the left of C, on the “n” of “Notifications”.

Why a gray line becomes a loop. A pixel closer than 0.15 to C, at the distance d, reads 0.20 − d from C, on the other side. So a pixel very close to C reads almost 0.20 from C: on the orange circle. So both places where a gray line crosses the orange circle show up right next to C. The part of the gray line between these two places comes out as a loop that starts and ends at C. Four gray lines cross the orange circle, so the middle shows four loops (pixels 3 to 6). A gray line close to C gives a big loop: the gray line below “Notifications” is 0.5325 − 0.50 = 0.0325 from C and gives the big gray loop above C (pixel 3). The gray line below “Bluetooth” is 0.50 − 0.3625 = 0.1375 from C and gives the small gray loop below C (pixel 6).

Why pixel 7 is not turned around. Pixel 7 is near the glass edge, farther than 0.15 from C, so its falloff is below 1 and its push is shorter than 0.20. Its pink arrow is shorter than its distance to C, so the arrow stops before C. Pixel 7 reads on its own side of C, so this copy of “Haptics” is not turned around. The places inside the orange circle show up twice: once turned around near C, and once not turned around near the glass edge. “Haptics” lies inside the orange circle, so it shows up twice (pixels 1 and 7). Right next to C, a few more letters come out very small: “ata” (the end of “Mobile Data”) and “&” (from “Sounds & Haptics”). They lie just inside the orange circle, so they land right next to C.

Section 5 · short code excerptThe iPhone shader

The real Metal function from the LiquidGlassLab app: chapter09/EdgeSafety.metal (explorer screen: chapter09/EdgeSafetyExplorer.swift). The parameter list is packed onto fewer lines here, and the comments on the right were added for the lesson. The two new lines are green.

[[ stitchable ]] half4 edgeSafeRefract(
    float2 position, half4 color, float2 resolution, float2 center,
    float dispLen, float radius, float band, float useClamp, float scale
) {
    float2 fragCoord = position * scale;
    float2 uv = fragCoord / resolution;
    float2 p = uv - center;                      // new: the glass center is a parameter
    float d = length(p);
    float2 disp = float2(0.0, 0.0);
    if (d > 0.0001) {
        float sd = d - radius;
        float falloff = 1.0 - smoothstep(-band, band, sd);
        disp = (p / d) * dispLen * falloff;
    }
    float2 readUv = uv - disp;
    if (useClamp > 0.5) {                        // the explorer's no fix / clamp switch
        readUv = clamp(readUv, 0.0, 1.0);        // new: the one line of this lesson
    }
    return checkerWithEdge(readUv);              // transparent outside 0 … 1
}
  • p = uv − center moves the glass. It is the same move as pB = p − B in Lesson 9.6.
  • clamp(readUv, 0.0, 1.0) gets a pair and two single numbers. Metal then clamps x and y separately, both into 0 … 1. The app build compiles this form.
  • checkerWithEdge is the Lesson 9.10 checkerboard with an edge: it returns half4(0.0, 0.0, 0.0, 0.0) when x or y is below 0 or above 1. It uses min(floor(y × 4.0), 3.0), so the edge y = 1.00 belongs to the last row.
Browser and iPhone. On this page, JavaScript runs the same chain once for every pixel of the canvas. On iPhone, Metal runs edgeSafeRefract once for every pixel of the view. One difference on purpose: our wallpaper is a function (Lesson 9.7), and a function can give a color anywhere, even at y = 1.06. A real picture — a photo, or the screen behind the glass — stops at its edge. So in this lesson the function returns transparent outside 0 … 1, like a real picture.

What SwiftUI does. SwiftUI's own sampler for a view's pixels (Layer.sample, a later chapter) already clamps before it reads. Its header file in Xcode, SwiftUI_Metal.h, contains the line p = metal::clamp(p, info[3], info[4]);. The header does not say what info[3] and info[4] hold. Our estimate: the two corners of the view's pixel area. The line itself proves the clamp.

Exercise 1 — is R′ where the push arrow crosses the edge?

The pink push arrow goes from W = (0.59, 0.98) to R = (0.53, 1.06). It crosses the yellow line on its way. Is R′ = clamp(R) the point where the arrow crosses the yellow line?

Show the answer

No. The arrow crosses the yellow line at E; R′ is a different point.

The arrow drops from y = 0.98 to y = 1.06: drop = 1.06 − 0.98 = 0.08 W is above the yellow line by: gap = 1.00 − 0.98 = 0.02 share of the arrow before the line: share = 0.02 ÷ 0.08 = 0.25 the arrow moves left by 0.06 in total: left = 0.25 × 0.06 = 0.015 E.x = 0.59 − 0.015 = 0.575 E = (0.575, 1.00) R′ = (0.53, 1.00) E.x − R′.x = 0.575 − 0.53 = 0.045

Why: clamp does not know the arrow. Clamp sees only R. R.x = 0.53 is inside 0 … 1, so R.x stays 0.53. Only R.y changes. R′ is straight above R.

Exercise 2 — a read point past a corner

A read point is R = (1.04, 1.06): right of the wallpaper and below it. Where is R′?

Show the answer
x: max(1.04, 0) = 1.04 x: min(1.04, 1) = 1.00 y: max(1.06, 0) = 1.06 y: min(1.06, 1) = 1.00 R′ = (1.00, 1.00)

R′ is the bottom-right corner. Both numbers are outside 0 … 1, so both change. The nearest point of the wallpaper to R is its corner.

Exercise 3 — the glass back in the middle

Put the glass back where Lesson 9.11 had it: C = (0.50, 0.50). With the clamp line in the shader, does any pixel change?

Show the answer

No pixel changes. The picture shows 16 pixels and their pink push arrows. Every arrow points toward C and ends inside the wallpaper.

Why: a push arrow is at most 0.10 long (dispLen). C is 0.50 away from every edge. An arrow ends either between its pixel and C, or at most 0.10 past C. Both places are inside the wallpaper. Clamp only moves read points that are outside, so here clamp changes nothing. That is why the clamp line can always stay in the shader.
