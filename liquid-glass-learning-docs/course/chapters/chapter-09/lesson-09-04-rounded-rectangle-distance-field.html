Chapter 9 · Signed distance to a rounded rectangle

Rounded rectangle distance field

Question: Lesson 9.2 already paints a circle by computing one number at every pixel: how far that pixel is from the visual center, minus the circle’s radius. Minus means inside. Zero means on the boundary. Plus means outside. Lesson 4.2 already drew a different shape: a rounded rectangle, with four straight sides and four quarter-circle corners. How do you compute that same kind of signed number, but to the rounded-rectangle boundary instead of to a circle?

A rounded-rectangle distance field is that signed number at every pixel. Orange in the picture is inside. Navy is outside. The green outline is where the number is 0: the rounded-rectangle boundary. You cannot reuse length(p) − radius. That measurement always describes a circle around the visual center. This lesson grows a smaller sharp rectangle, the inner box, by the corner radius.
Complete. This page is a JavaScript stand-in. The iPhone 16 Pro explorer’s stitchable function grows the inner box by the corner radius and tests the sign. Default outer half-size is (0.30, 0.20), so the full rectangle is 0.60 wide and 0.40 tall, not 0.60 by 0.30. Default corner radius is 0.10. Section 4 shows why the teaching pixel is exactly p = (0.26, −0.18).

Canonical wording: signed distance field, construction-circle center, inner box, abs, edge leftover, signed distance.

1 · WHY NOT THE CIRCLE FORMULA

length(p) is still the distance from C. That always draws a circle.

Lesson 9.1 defined length(p): the straight-line length from the visual center C to this pixel. Lesson 9.2 subtracted a radius from that length. The pixels where that result equals 0 sit on a circle around C. A rounded rectangle is not a circle. It has long straight sides. So this lesson does not ask length(p) for the signed distance.

length(p) is the pink segment: C to the pixel. Subtract a radius and the boundary is a circle around C. That is the whole story for a circle. It is not the story for a rounded rectangle.

The orange outline is the rounded rectangle. A circle of radius 0.20 from C hits the top and bottom, then misses the left and right. A circle of radius 0.30 hits the left and right, then overshoots the top and bottom. Neither circle is this shape.

2 · NAME EVERY OBJECT

C is the visual center. I is a construction-circle center. They are different points.

Lesson 4.2 already named these objects on a rounded rectangle. This picture puts them in this lesson’s p units, measured from the visual center. Short letters sit on the drawing. The cards on the right are the full names.

C · visual center

The origin of the pair p. This pixel has p = (0, 0). The draw rectangle does not move. C is not a corner center.

p = (0, 0)

Outer sharp box

The axis-aligned rectangle that still owns the leftmost, topmost, rightmost, and bottommost positions. Half-size is center-to-edge, not the full size.

half (0.30, 0.20) → full 0.60 × 0.40

Right straight edge

The vertical straight piece of the rounded boundary. It sits at x = +0.30. “Flat” in later formulas means this kind of straight piece, not an arc.

x = +0.30, from join R down to the bottom-right join

I · construction-circle center

Lesson 4.2’s curve center for one corner. Sit one corner radius in from both meeting outer edges. The dashed green circle around I has radius 0.10. Only the outer quarter of that circle is the rounded corner.

top-right I = (0.20, −0.10)

Inner box

The smaller sharp rectangle whose four corners are the four construction-circle centers. Growing every inner-box boundary point outward by 0.10 produces the orange outline.

inner half (0.20, 0.10) → full 0.40 × 0.20

Join points T and R

Where a straight outer edge ends and the quarter-circle begins. T is on the top edge. R is on the right edge.

T = (0.20, −0.20) · R = (0.30, −0.10)

p · teaching pixel

One pixel chosen to sit on the top-right quarter-circle, with countable arithmetic. Section 4 builds its coordinates from I.

p = (0.26, −0.18)

Pink q, gray length(p)

The pink segment is I to p. That length is 0.10. That is the analog of Lesson 9.2’s length(p) in this corner: distance from the construction-circle center, not from C. The gray dashed segment is C to p. That length is about 0.316. Production does not use it.

sd analog = pink − radius, not gray − radius
Full size is 0.60 × 0.40. Half-width 0.30 means the right edge is 0.30 from C, so the left-to-right span is 0.60. Half-height 0.20 means the top edge is 0.20 from C, so the top-to-bottom span is 0.40. The corner radius 0.10 is not added to the height. It only replaces each sharp corner with a quarter-circle inside that same 0.60 × 0.40 box.
3 · GROW THE INNER BOX

Move every inner-box boundary point out by 0.10.

Lesson 9.2 grew a point: every direction from C by the same radius, which is a circle. This lesson grows a rectangle. A flat side stays flat because every point on that side moves straight out. A corner swings through a quarter-circle because the nearest inner-box point is the corner point I.

Signed distance is I to p, then minus r

On the quarter-circle, the nearest inner-box point is I. Distance from p to I is 0.10. Subtract the same 0.10 and you get 0. That 0 means on the boundary. C is not on that measurement. A line from C to p is unused.

Two subtracts, two jobs

First subtract: innerHalf = outerHalf − 0.10 places I. That builds the inner box. Second subtract: sd = (distance to the inner box) − 0.10 moves the zero out onto the orange outline.

The leftover square is outside

The outer sharp corner (0.30, −0.20) is the pink square in section 2. The quarter-circle never reaches it. Distance from that corner to I is about 0.141, which is larger than 0.10, so that pixel is outside. Production stores navy there.

4 · WHY p = (0.26, −0.18)

(0.6, 0.8) is a direction of length 1. It is not a pixel.

Any pixel on the quarter-circle has signed distance 0. This lesson picks one whose arithmetic you can check by hand, using the same 3-4-5 triangle as Lesson 9.1.

0.6 and 0.8 are the two legs of a 3-4-5 triangle scaled so the long side is 1. Check: 0.6 × 0.6 + 0.8 × 0.8 = 0.36 + 0.64 = 1. This pair is a direction, like an arrow of length 1. It is not the teaching pixel and not a position on the rectangle.

Multiply the unit arrow by the corner radius: 0.10 × (0.6, 0.8) = (0.06, 0.08). That new arrow has length 0.10. Add it to the inner corner in the all-positive quarter after abs: (0.20, 0.10) + (0.06, 0.08) = (0.26, 0.18). The real teaching pixel is the top-right mirror, so p.y flips sign: (0.26, −0.18).

inner corner after abs, called I: (outerHalf.x − r, outerHalf.y − r) = (0.30 − 0.10, 0.20 − 0.10) = (0.20, 0.10) unit direction of length 1: (0.6, 0.8) because 0.36 + 0.64 = 1 (3-4-5 triangle: 3/5 and 4/5) radius-long step: 0.10 × (0.6, 0.8) = (0.06, 0.08)
mirrored pixel after abs: (0.20, 0.10) + (0.06, 0.08) = (0.26, 0.18) actual teaching pixel, top-right quarter: p = (0.26, −0.18) q = abs(p) − innerHalf = (0.26, 0.18) − (0.20, 0.10) = (0.06, 0.08) length(q) = 0.10 = r, so sd = 0
What q is. After folding with abs, q is the pair of leftover distances past the inner-box edges. In this corner both leftovers are positive, so the nearest inner-box point is I, and length(q) is the straight-line length of the pink triangle’s long side. That is the analog of Lesson 9.2’s length(p), except the triangle is built from I, not from C. Then sd = length(q) − 0.10, the same subtract as the circle, now from that inner-corner length.

7 · iOS ROUTE
Three shader arguments, four branches, one subtract

    

      
[[ stitchable ]] half4 roundedRectSignedDistance(
    float2 position,
    half4 color,
    float2 resolution,
    float scale,
    float halfWidth,
    float halfHeight,
    float cornerRadius
) {
    float2 fragCoord = position * scale;
    float2 uv = fragCoord / resolution;
    float2 p = uv - float2(0.5, 0.5);
    float2 q = abs(p) - float2(
        halfWidth - cornerRadius,
        halfHeight - cornerRadius
    );
    float sdBox;
    if (q.x > 0.0) {
        if (q.y > 0.0) {
            sdBox = length(q);
        } else {
            sdBox = q.x;
        }
    } else {
        if (q.y > 0.0) {
            sdBox = q.y;
        } else {
            sdBox = q.x > q.y ? q.x : q.y;
        }
    }
    float sd = sdBox - cornerRadius;
    half4 navy = half4(0.10, 0.25, 0.40, 1.0);
    half4 orange = half4(0.88, 0.48, 0.18, 1.0);
    if (sd < 0.0) {
        return orange;
    }
    return navy;
}

private let panelSizePt: CGFloat = 360
private let halfWidth: Double = 0.30
private let halfHeight: Double = 0.20
private let teachPx: Double = 0.26
private let teachPy: Double = -0.18

        .float2(pixelWidth, pixelHeight),

    )

What each part changes

        
halfWidth and halfHeight are the outer half-size. Subtracting cornerRadius from each places the inner box. q is how far the mirrored pixel sits past each inner edge.

        
The nested if picks the nearest inner-box point. Both leftovers positive: that point is I, so length(q). Only one leftover positive: that leftover is the distance to the matching inner flat. Both negative: the pixel is inside the inner box, and the leftover nearer zero (the larger of the two negatives) is the distance.

        
cornerRadius = 0.10 so the teaching pixel’s sd is exactly 0. panelSizePt = 360 stays square so one UV step right equals one UV step down in pixels. The two half-sizes still differ, so the silhouette is a rectangle.

        
Xcode compiles roundedRectSignedDistance in the .metal file. ShaderLibrary looks it up. position is points, so fragCoord = position * scale and resolution are both in pixels. scale is displayScale. pixelWidth and pixelHeight are 360 × displayScale. halfWidth, halfHeight, and cornerRadius are in p units. There is no runtime string compiler. Moving the slider passes a new .float. That is Lesson 8.9’s argument write. Pass every argument. Leaving one out still compiles the Swift file, and SwiftUI refuses that draw.

        
The green overlay is a SwiftUI Canvas rounded-rect stroke, not a second shader.

10 · EXERCISES
Answer before opening each hidden solution

    
At the visual center C, r = 0.10, what is sd, and which color does production store?
q = (−0.20, −0.10). Both leftovers are negative, so sdBox is the larger, −0.10. sd = −0.10 − 0.10 = −0.20. Minus means inside, so the hard if stores orange. That −0.20 is also minus the outer half-height: C is 0.20 inside the nearer outer flat.

    
Diagnostic: someone writes float sd = length(p) − cornerRadius. What shape is the set of pixels where sd = 0, and why is the leftover square then wrong?
Pixels where sd = 0 form a circle of radius 0.10 around C. The teaching shape’s outer straight edges sit at 0.30 and 0.20, so that circle is much smaller than the rounded rectangle. The leftover-square pixel would be classified by distance from C, not by distance from I to the quarter-circle.

    
The leftover-square pixel is inside the outer sharp box. Why does production store navy?
Inside the outer sharp box is not the test. The test is sd of the rounded rectangle. q = (0.10, 0.10), length(q) = 0.14142, sd = +0.04142. Plus means outside the quarter-circle. The leftover square between the sharp corner and the arc is outside the shape.

    
If corner radius is 0, what is the inner box, and what silhouette do you get?
innerHalf = outerHalf. The inner box is the outer sharp box. Growing by 0 leaves that sharp rectangle. Production stores orange inside that rectangle, including the four corner squares that a positive radius would leave as navy leftovers.
