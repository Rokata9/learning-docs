Lesson 9.14 — Lens checkpoint

A round glass lens with its own outline, over a wallpaper that moves. The last lesson of Chapter 9. Light theme · desktop · works from file://.

In Lessons 9.11 to 9.13 every pixel W got a read point: the point of the wallpaper where W takes its color. That is one answer per pixel: where does W read? But the glass had no edge of its own. The bend faded out, and the page had to draw a violet circle to show where the glass is. A real lens ends at a sharp outline. This lesson gives every pixel a second, separate answer: is W glass? The mask of Lesson 9.3 gives this second answer. The last line of the shader combines the two answers. Under the lens the wallpaper moves. That makes the wallpaper a test pattern: a simple picture made to reveal mistakes. Here it shows what moves and what stays still.

  • The wallpaper: the black-and-white checkerboard of Lesson 9.13. It moves to the left, 0.10 of the screen width every second.
  • The violet circle (left picture only): where the glass is. Center (the violet dot) C = (0.40, 0.54), radius 0.25. The right picture needs no circle: the glass shows its own outline.

Chapter 9 ends with a checkpoint question: why are the mask and the read points two separate problems? The box at the end of Section 2 answers it.

Section 2 · the new linesThe second answer in the code

Lesson 9.13 returned the color from its three read points. That color now gets a name, bent, and three new lines follow it:

sd = length(p) − radius both answers start from this one number falloff = 1 − smoothstep(−band, band, sd) answer 2 (Lesson 9.11): how long the push is, band = 0.10 bent = half4(colRed.r, colGreen.g, colBlue.b, 1) answer 2 (Lessons 9.12, 9.13): the color from the three read points mask = 1 − smoothstep(−maskBand, maskBand, sd) new, answer 1: how much this pixel is glass, maskBand = 0.002 plain = wall(uv, time) new: the wallpaper at the pixel itself, as if there were no glass color = mix(plain, bent, mask) new: the mask picks between plain and bent
  • mask is the soft mask of Lesson 9.3 with a much thinner band: maskBand = 0.002, about one pixel of the pictures on this page. So mask is 1 inside the circle, 0 outside, and 0.5 exactly on the outline.
  • plain is the wallpaper at the pixel's own position uv. It needs no push and no clamp, because uv is always on the screen.
  • wall(x, y, time) is the moving wallpaper. The three read points ask it too. time appears only inside wall.
  • mix(a, b, m) = a + (b − a) × m. With m = 0 it gives plain; with m = 1 it gives bent. It works on each channel separately.

Why not reuse falloff?

The shader already has a number that is 1 inside the glass and 0 outside: falloff. Why compute a second one? Switch the number that the mix uses, on the same output. The magnifier enlarges the pixels inside the lime box 4 times, so every pixel of the picture is a 4 × 4 square.

Why falloff fails. Falloff must go from 1 to 0 slowly, over sd = −0.10 … +0.10, because the bend must fade without a corner (Lesson 9.11). But a glass outline is sharp. Mixed by falloff, every pixel in that 0.20-wide ring is part plain and part bent: two faded copies of each edge, one straight and one bent. The outline needs a band of about one pixel; the bend needs a band of 0.20. One band cannot do both jobs.

Why not a hard if. sd < 0 ? 1 : 0 has no in-between values (Lesson 9.3). Each pixel on the outline is fully bent or fully plain, so the outline shows stair steps one pixel high (the magnifier). With maskBand = 0.002, the pixels on the outline get in-between values and the steps disappear. The steps show only where the two sides of the outline have different colors. Where both sides are white, no outline shows at all.

Chapter 9 checkpoint · why two separate problems

  1. They answer different questions. The mask says how much a pixel is glass: one number, used by mix. The read points say where a pixel reads: points, used to ask the wallpaper.
  2. They need different widths. Both start from the same sd. The outline needs a band of about one pixel (0.004 wide). The bend needs a band of 0.20. The comparison above shows what happens when one number does both jobs.
  3. Neither one uses the other, the wallpaper, or the time. So the wallpaper can move while the outline and every read point stay still (the pictures at the top). A mistake in one answer is fixed in its own line (Exercise 3).

Section 5 · short code excerptThe iPhone shader

The real Metal functions from the LiquidGlassLab app: chapter09/LensCheckpoint.metal (explorer screen: chapter09/LensCheckpointExplorer.swift). The parameter list is packed onto fewer lines here, and the comments on the right were added for the lesson. The new lines are green.

[[ stitchable ]] half4 glassLens(
    float2 position, half4 color, float2 resolution, float2 center,
    float dispLen, float radius, float band, float spread,
    float maskBand, float time, float speed, float scale
) {
    float2 fragCoord = position * scale;
    float2 uv = fragCoord / resolution;
    float2 p = uv - center;
    float d = length(p);
    float sd = d - radius;                                          // moved out of the if: the mask needs sd for every pixel
    float2 disp = float2(0.0, 0.0);
    if (d > 0.0001) {
        float falloff = 1.0 - smoothstep(-band, band, sd);
        disp = (p / d) * dispLen * falloff;
    }
    float2 readRed   = clamp(uv - disp * (1.0 - spread), 0.0, 1.0);
    float2 readGreen = clamp(uv - disp, 0.0, 1.0);
    float2 readBlue  = clamp(uv - disp * (1.0 + spread), 0.0, 1.0);
    half4 colRed   = movingChecker(readRed, time, speed);
    half4 colGreen = movingChecker(readGreen, time, speed);
    half4 colBlue  = movingChecker(readBlue, time, speed);
    half4 bent  = half4(colRed.r, colGreen.g, colBlue.b, 1.0);      // the Lesson 9.13 color, now with a name
    float mask  = 1.0 - smoothstep(-maskBand, maskBand, sd);        // new: how much this pixel is glass
    half4 plain = movingChecker(uv, time, speed);                   // new: the wallpaper at the pixel itself
    return mix(plain, bent, half(mask));                            // new: the mask picks
}

half4 movingChecker(float2 uv, float time, float speed) {
    if (uv.x < 0.0 || uv.x > 1.0 || uv.y < 0.0 || uv.y > 1.0) {
        return half4(0.0, 0.0, 0.0, 0.0);                           // a real picture stops at its edge (Lesson 9.12)
    }
    float col = floor((uv.x + speed * time) * 4.0);                 // new: + speed × time moves the wallpaper left
    float row = min(floor(uv.y * 4.0), 3.0);
    bool even = fmod(col + row, 2.0) < 0.5;
    return even ? half4(1.0, 1.0, 1.0, 1.0) : half4(0.0, 0.0, 0.0, 1.0);
}
  • sd moved out of the if. The if guards only the push, which divides by d. The mask needs sd for every pixel, also the pixel at C, where the push is skipped.
  • half(mask) turns the float into a half, the same number type as the colors (Lesson 8.7), so mix gets three values of one kind.
  • Both lines use floor, which rounds down. Row 0 is y from 0 to 0.25: y × 4 runs from 0 to just under 1, and floor makes that 0. So the rows are numbered 0, 1, 2, 3, not 1 to 4. Only row has min(…, 3.0) (the smaller of the two numbers), because y stops at the bottom edge, y = 1.00, and that edge gives row 4, a row that does not exist. The edge is read often: the clamp puts every read point below the screen exactly on y = 1.00 (Lesson 9.12, and Exercise 3 below). Example: the read point (0.40, 1.00) at time = 2 s, with speed = 0.10.
    shift = speed × time = 0.10 × 2 = 0.20 x + shift = 0.40 + 0.20 = 0.60 col: 0.60 × 4 = 2.40 → floor → 2 row: 1.00 × 4 = 4.00 → floor → 4 without min: col + row = 2 + 4 = 6 → even → white (wrong) with min: min(4, 3) = 3 → col + row = 2 + 3 = 5 → odd → black, the color of row 3 in column 2
    Row 4 is one more than row 3, so col + row is one more too: even becomes odd, and odd becomes even. So without min, every read point on the edge would get the opposite color of the bottom row.
  • col has no min(…, 3.0), because col uses x + shift, and the shift grows with time. So x + shift goes past 1.00, and col goes past 3. Example: the pixel (0.90, 0.10) at time = 2 s.
    shift = speed × time = 0.10 × 2 = 0.20 x + shift = 0.90 + 0.20 = 1.10 col: 1.10 × 4 = 4.40 → floor → 4 row: 0.10 × 4 = 0.40 → floor → 0 col + row = 4 + 0 = 4 → even → white with min on col: min(4, 3) = 3 → col + row = 3 + 0 = 3 → odd → black (wrong)
    Column 4 is real: it is the next column of the checkerboard, sliding in from the right. At time = 2 s it covers x from 0.80 to 1.00: it starts where (x + shift) × 4 = 4, that is x + shift = 1.00, so x = 1.00 − 0.20 = 0.80. With min on col, every pixel to the right of column 3 would copy column 3, so no new square would come in.
  • fmod(col + row, 2.0) is the remainder after dividing by 2: 0 for even, 1 for odd.
  • In a Metal file a function must be declared before a function that calls it. In the real file, movingChecker comes first.
Browser and iPhone. On this page, JavaScript runs the same chain for every pixel of a canvas, about 30 times per second while a picture plays. On iPhone, the explorer wraps the view in TimelineView(.animation), the frame clock of Lesson 5.2, and passes the elapsed seconds as the time argument, as in Lesson 8.10. Metal then runs glassLens for every pixel of the view, about once per display frame.

Two iPhone details. The explorer passes maskBand = 1 ÷ resolution.x: one device pixel in UV, the same idea as 0.002 on this page. It also passes the time modulo 5 seconds: after 5 s the wallpaper has moved two cells (0.10 × 5 = 0.50) and looks the same again, and a small float stays exact.

The cost. Every pixel asks the wallpaper four times: three read points and plain. Where mask is 0, bent is not used. A production shader can return plain early there, or run only on the glass's own rectangle.

Exercise 2 — a capsule mask with the circle's push: which pixel differs from the wallpaper?

The change. A classmate wants glass shaped like a capsule. They change only the mask line: it now uses the capsule's sd instead of the circle's. They do not change the lines that compute the read points, so the push still comes from the circle around C. That push is 0 outside the orange ring.

What this checks. The shader answers two separate questions for each pixel. The mask line says whether the pixel is glass. The read-point lines say whether its read points move (the push). This exercise checks what a pixel shows when only the mask line changes. We ask because giving the glass a new shape means changing these lines.

What you do. For T and for M, read two facts off the picture. Inside the violet capsule? Then mask = 1: the pixel is glass. Inside the orange ring? Then the pixel has a push: its read points move. Then use the last line, mix(plain, bent, mask). It gives plain, the wallpaper under the pixel, when mask = 0. It gives bent, the color at the three read points, when mask = 1.

Why: a pixel can show something other than the wallpaper under it only when it is glass and has a push. That is only inside both outlines (“here: glass and a push” in the right picture). The new mask changed the outline, but the read points still follow the circle. At the ends of the capsule the circle gives no push, so the glass cannot be seen there (as in Exercise 1). Above and below the capsule, the mask throws the circle's push away.

What to remember: to give the glass a new shape, change both answers: the mask and the push. Changing only the mask line gives a capsule outline with a circle's bend inside it. This lesson does not build a push for a capsule, and this exercise does not need one. Lesson 10.3 (the surface normal) builds a push for any shape.

W₃ = (0.59, 0.98), C = (0.50, 1.10) p = W₃ − C = (0.59 − 0.50, 0.98 − 1.10) = (0.09, −0.12) p.x² = 0.09 × 0.09 = 0.0081 p.y² = 0.12 × 0.12 = 0.0144 p.x² + p.y² = 0.0081 + 0.0144 = 0.0225 d = √0.0225 = 0.15 mask: sd = d − radius = 0.15 − 0.25 = −0.10 −0.10 ≤ −0.002 → mask = 1: W₃ is glass push: falloff = 1 − smoothstep(−0.10, 0.10, −0.10) = 1 − 0 = 1 (sd = −0.10 is where the band starts) push = 0.10 × 1 = 0.10 dir = p ÷ d: 0.09 ÷ 0.15 = 0.60, −0.12 ÷ 0.15 = −0.80 disp = dir × push: 0.60 × 0.10 = 0.06, −0.80 × 0.10 = −0.08 readRed: 1 − spread = 1 − 0.10 = 0.90 (red moves 0.90 of the push) disp × 0.90 = (0.06 × 0.90, −0.08 × 0.90) = (0.054, −0.072) W₃ − disp × 0.90 = (0.59 − 0.054, 0.98 − (−0.072)) = (0.536, 1.052) readGreen: W₃ − disp = (0.59 − 0.06, 0.98 − (−0.08)) = (0.53, 1.06) readBlue: 1 + spread = 1 + 0.10 = 1.10 (blue moves 1.10 of the push) disp × 1.10 = (0.06 × 1.10, −0.08 × 1.10) = (0.066, −0.088) W₃ − disp × 1.10 = (0.59 − 0.066, 0.98 − (−0.088)) = (0.524, 1.068) no clamp: all three y are more than 1.00: below the screen, no wallpaper → every channel = 0 → bent = (0, 0, 0) mask = 1 → W₃ stores bent: black clamp: y = min(1.052, 1.00) = 1.00 (the same for 1.06 and 1.068); every x is already inside 0 … 1 col: 0.536 × 4 = 2.144, 0.53 × 4 = 2.12, 0.524 × 4 = 2.096 → floor → 2 (all three) row: 1.00 × 4 = 4.00 → floor → 4 → min(4, 3) = 3 (the edge belongs to the last row) col + row = 2 + 3 = 5 → odd → navy (all three) → W₃ stores navy

Why: the mask can only choose between plain and bent. W₃ is well inside the outline, so it must show bent. Its bent is black because its read points are below the screen: that is a read-point problem, so the fix is a read-point line, the clamp of Lesson 9.12. A mask of 0 in the strip would cut a piece out of the glass. The strip is black, not see-through as in Lesson 9.12, because the Lesson 9.13 line sets alpha to 1.0, and every channel read outside the wallpaper is 0.
