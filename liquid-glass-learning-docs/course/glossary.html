Canonical definitions

Liquid Glass glossary

Terms are defined here once. Lessons link back when a term returns. The wording will grow as the course introduces more precise models.

Transparent

A transparent area lets the background pass through with no added visible material.

Visible test: the background looks unchanged.

Translucent

A translucent area shows the background and also adds some foreground color.

Visible test: the background remains recognizable, but its colors change.

Blur

Blur makes fine detail less sharp by combining information from nearby positions.

Visible test: a thin edge spreads into a softer band.

Diffusion

Diffusion is visible light spreading inside a material. User interfaces often imitate it with blur and other softening operations.

Visible test: detail becomes softer and less distinct.

Refraction

Refraction is a change in the apparent position or direction of background detail as light passes through a material.

Visible test: a straight line bends, shifts, or changes size.

Tint

A tint adds a controlled color influence without fully replacing the background.

Visible test: many background colors move toward the same added hue.

Highlight

A highlight is a bright region that suggests reflected light. A specular highlight is a concentrated bright reflection.

Visible test: one edge or spot becomes brighter than the rest.

Shadow

A shadow is a darker region outside or below a surface. It suggests separation from the content behind it.

Visible test: the surface appears raised or detached.

Morph

A morph is a continuous change from one shape into another shape.

Visible test: width, height, corners, or outline change without a sudden replacement.

Implementation hypothesis

An implementation hypothesis is a possible explanation for how software produced a visible result. It is not proven by watching the result.

Evidence needed: documentation, source code, tracing, profiling, or direct measurement.

Frame

A frame is one complete image shown by the screen at one moment.

Visible test: pause a recording. The single paused image is one frame.

Transition

A transition is the sequence of visible changes between one stable state and another stable state.

Visible test: identify the starting state, the ending state, and the changing images between them.

Stage

A stage is a human-chosen name for a useful group of nearby frames that share a visible purpose.

Important: the real animation does not need to pause at a stage boundary.
