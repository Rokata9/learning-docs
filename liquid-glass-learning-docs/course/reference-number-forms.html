Reference — how the course writes numbers

Every way the lessons write a point, an arrow, a view of an arrow, a color or a scale, each with an example read in words. Lessons link here instead of repeating a coordinate panel.

  • A point has x, y and height: where it is, counted from the origin of its coordinates.
  • An arrow has an x part, a y part and a height part: how far it goes, counted from its own start.
  • How to read a part:
    • a plus number: the arrow goes the way the column heading says;
    • a minus number: it goes the opposite way: left, toward the top of the picture, or down;
    • 0: it does not go that way at all.
  • Each picture names its coordinates in a label inside it (in a 3D picture: the first line of the corner box).

Two or three numbers in brackets can mean four different things. The words just before the brackets say which. One trap, in Lesson 10.3: in board units P = (0, 0, 0), so the end of a walk from P prints the same three numbers as the walk. On the glass they differ.
