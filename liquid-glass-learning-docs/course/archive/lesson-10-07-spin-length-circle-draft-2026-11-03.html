Lesson 10.7 — The spin keeps the length: T goes round a circle

Spin the teal line around the x line: its end T stays the same distance from Gx. Light theme · desktop · works from file://.

In Lesson 10.6 the spin turned the teal line around the x line and kept it on the wall. This lesson shows that the spin also keeps the teal line's length, so its end T goes round a circle on the wall, through the tip of N. Lesson 10.10 needs that fixed length to show a square corner.

Before you start

Used from the course

  • Lesson 10.5: the wall is flat; the straight line from Gx to the tip of N lies on it.
  • Lesson 10.4: the x leg from S to Gx; the x line; the wall: all points 0.302 to the right of S; the wall seen straight on (Step 5); arrow units (1 = the length of N).
  • Lesson 10.3: N at the rim pixel K = (0.62, 0.66); a turn keeps lengths (Step 6); a quarter turn (Step 7).
  • Lesson 9.2: a circle: all points at one distance from its middle. Lesson 9.1: a right triangle's long side² = leg² + leg².
  • Lesson 10.2 and Lesson 10.1: only inside the ⓘ chains of N and S.
New here
  • The wall seen straight on draws Gx to T at its whole length. Step 2.
  • The one idea: seen straight on, the spin is a turn around Gx, and a turn keeps lengths, so Gx to T stays 0.953 at every spin (Step 3).
  • So T's path is a circle around Gx on the wall (Step 4), and the tip of N is on it (Step 5).

Exercise 1 — Which of the points a, b and c on the wall can T reach at some spin?

Why this question. All three points are on the wall, like T. But being on the wall is not enough for T to get there.

What you do. For each point, find how far it is from Gx: its y part and its height part meet at a square corner (Lesson 9.1). Compare with Gx to T.

Question: which of the points a, b and c on the wall can T reach at some spin?

Show the answer
  • T's circle: every point of the wall 0.953 from Gx.
  • Point a, from Gx:
    • y part² = (−0.5) × (−0.5) = 0.25
    • height part² = 0.5 × 0.5 = 0.25
    • length² = 0.25 + 0.25 = 0.5
    • length = √0.5 = 0.707
    • Inside the circle.
  • Point b, from Gx:
    • y part² = 0.864 × 0.864 = 0.746496
    • height part² = 0.402 × 0.402 = 0.161604
    • length² = 0.746496 + 0.161604 = 0.9081
    • length = √0.9081 = 0.953
    • On the circle.
  • Point c, from Gx:
    • y part² = (−0.8) × (−0.8) = 0.64
    • height part² = 0.8 × 0.8 = 0.64
    • length² = 0.64 + 0.64 = 1.28
    • length = √1.28 = 1.131
    • Outside the circle.
  • Points a and c lie on one line through Gx. At one spin the teal line lies along that line, but T is then 0.953 from Gx: past point a, short of point c.
  • So only point b: at a spin of about 25°, T is there.
Exercise 2 — The teal line has spun 200°. How far from Gx is T now?

Why this question. In this angled drawing the teal line now looks shorter than at its start. Has the spin made it shorter?

What you do. Remember what the spin does in the wall seen straight on (Step 3), and what that drawing shows at its whole length (Step 2).

Question: the teal line has spun 200°. How far from Gx is T now?

Show the answer
  • Seen straight on, the spin is a turn around Gx.
  • A turn keeps lengths: the teal line is drawn 0.953 long at 0° and at 200°.
  • This drawing shows Gx to T at its whole length.
  • So T is still 0.953 from Gx, on its circle.
