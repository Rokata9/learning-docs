Lesson 10.46 — Which way is out: the outward direction at a pixel

Follows the system’s light or dark setting · desktop · works from file://.

Why: the bright line along the menu's outline and the lens both need, at every pixel, outward: an arrow 1 pt long that points straight out of the glass. The shader knows only sd: a pixel's distance from the outline, with a minus inside.

The idea: the shader does not know where the outline is, only sd. But straight out is the way in which sd grows the most, by 1 for each 1 pt. The shader works out how much sd changes over 1 pt to the right and over 1 pt toward the bottom, and takes the two changes as the x part and the y part of one arrow. In any direction, sd grows by the dot product of that arrow and the direction, so it grows the most along the arrow itself: the arrow points straight out. Divided by its length, it is outward. At P, in the menu's top-left corner, the changes are −0.60 and −0.80. A minus x part goes to the left and a minus y part toward the top, so outward goes 0.60 pt to the left and 0.80 pt toward the top.

Where this is used. Lesson 10.47 lights the bright line with outward. Lesson 10.48 puts Section 4's code into the menu's Metal shader. Lesson 11.2 shifts each pixel's read point along the line of outward: the lens. From Lesson 12.1 on, the glass is two shapes joined softly: no single circle or edge gives the direction, but the four neighbors still do.

Uses

  • Lesson 9.2, Lesson 9.4: sd, the signed distance: minus inside, 0 on the outline, plus outside. In a rounded corner: the distance from the center of the corner's circle, minus the corner radius.
  • Lesson 9.1: a distance from its two legs, √(leg² + leg²); an arrow's length from its two parts, the same way.
  • Lesson 10.2, Lesson 10.3: the change of a number from A to B, divided by |AB|; the same from D to E. There: the glass's height. Here: sd.
  • Lesson 9.10: an arrow divided by its length is 1 long and keeps its direction.
  • Lesson 10.21, Lesson 10.23: dot(a, b) = a's x part × b's x part + a's y part × b's y part. With b 1 long, it is largest when b points the same way as a.
  • Lesson 10.42, Lesson 10.44: brightness = dot(N, L). An N twice as long gives twice the dot, so N must be 1 long.
