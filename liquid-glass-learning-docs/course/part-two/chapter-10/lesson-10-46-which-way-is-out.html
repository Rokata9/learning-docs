Lesson 10.46 — Which way is out?

You’ll work out which way the arrow points using distances from a few spots near P. The shader, the code that colors each pixel, can ask for those distances.

Where this is used. The rim line is the thin bright line around the glass. Lesson 10.47 uses the outward direction to tell which way the glass faces at its edge. Comparing that direction with the lamp’s direction helps determine where the rim line looks brightest.

Uses

  • A distance can carry a minus sign to mark a position inside an outline. Lesson 9.2 and Lesson 9.4.
  • Sideways and vertical distances give the length of a straight path between two points. Lesson 9.1.
  • Dividing both parts of an arrow by its length makes it one unit long. Lesson 9.10.

The diagrams use screen points (pt), a unit for positions and distances on the screen. Positions start at the top-left of the 402 × 874 pt screen. The first position number, x, counts right. The second, y, counts down. The drawings enlarge these distances. In the arithmetic, green marks an input, and ≈ marks a rounded result.

Section 1 · the idea in picturesFrom distances to an outward arrow

Build an outward arrow from signed-distance samples. Then use the arrow to see why two pixels equally far inside the rim can end up different shades.

    What the colored terms mean
    Straight distance
    The length of a straight line between two points. In the corner calculations it runs from K to the sample.
    Signed distance
    Distance from a sample to its nearest outline point, with a minus sign inside the glass.
    Change or rise
    Ending signed distance minus starting signed distance. A positive change is a rise.
    Rate
    Signed-distance change divided by the sample gap. It estimates the change per point of movement near P.
    Outward arrow
    The arrow built from the two rates and scaled to length 1. It tells the shader which way the glass faces.
    From U, move 1.00 pt to the right. Does signed distance rise, fall, or stay the same?
