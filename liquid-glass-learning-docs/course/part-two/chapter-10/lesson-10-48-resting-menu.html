Lesson 10.48 — Build: the resting menu’s outline, rim line and shadow

Build one Metal function that colors this dark menu. Follow P to see how the outline gives the menu a soft edge, how the rim line brightens the starting gray, and how a shifted copy of the outline darkens the page.

Where this is used. Lesson 11.1 prepares a glass picture with the page, the menu’s starting color, and the menu rows. The lens build in 11.4 reads that picture so nearby stripes or rows bend at the edge of the same resting outline.

What comes together

  • A signed distance says how far a pixel is from the outline. A soft mask turns that distance into a share from 0 to 1. Rounded rectangle · Soft mask
  • outwardAt finds which way is out. addRimLine uses that direction to work out how much white to add near the edge. Lesson 10.46 · Lesson 10.47
  • The shadow uses the same distance rule with a wider fade and a shifted read. Shifted read

Section 1 · worked buildOne pixel from outline to final color

Positions are in screen points (pt) on a 402 × 874 pt screen. The origin is the top-left corner. x increases to the right and y increases down. A device pixel is a tiny square the screen lights up. We use a 3 px-per-pt screen for the example. The drawings enlarge the screen so you can see the edge. In the arithmetic, green marks known inputs and ≈ marks a rounded result.

    Find P’s distance from the outline

    The menu’s measured center is (238.8, 742.6) pt. Its measured size is 261 × 188 pt and we model each corner as part of a circle of radius 36 pt. These values come from the resting Safari frame and are good to about ±4 pt. The shape code takes half the width and half the height so it can count out from the center.

    Half width = 261 ÷ 2 = 130.5 pt
    Half height = 188 ÷ 2 = 94 pt
    Left edge x = 238.8 − 130.5 = 108.3 pt
    Top edge y = 742.6 − 94 = 648.6 pt

    K is the center of the upper-left corner circle. Start at the left and top edges and move 36 pt inward along each axis.

    K.x = 108.3 + 36 = 144.3 pt
    K.y = 648.6 + 36 = 684.6 pt

    We choose P at (122.76, 655.88) pt as the center of a pixel near the outline. The straight distance from K to P comes from the two legs of the right triangle. The outline lies at the circle’s radius. Subtract that radius from the length to get signed distance (sd). A minus means inside, zero means on the outline, and a plus means outside.

    Give the menu a soft edge

    Step 1 put P 0.10 pt inside the outline. P marks the center of a pixel, and part of that pixel can still extend outside. We want the glass and the page to share that pixel so the outline looks smooth.

    Follow the amber line through P and K. It crosses the purple outline at Q. The close-up turns the amber line horizontal. The page is on the left and the menu is on the right. Each teal square shows one whole pixel crossing that boundary.

    The amber line across the square shows the strip the shader uses. It spans one pixel width. The close-up draws the edge straight and parallel to the sides of the square so you can see the shader’s estimate. In this drawing, the part of the strip inside the outline and the part of the square inside the outline take up the same fraction. We use a screen with 3 device pixels per point.

    Width of one pixel = 1 px ÷ 3 px per pt = ⅓ pt

    Start at Q. The outline cuts the square straight down the middle. Half of the square lies on the menu side, so the inside fraction is 0.50.

    Now use P. The square’s center is 0.10 pt inside. A narrow slice of the square still crosses onto the page. The move from Q to P puts 0.10 pt more of the strip on the glass side. Compare that gain with the whole width to find the fraction gained.

    Fraction gained = 0.10 pt ÷ (⅓ pt) = 0.30
    Fraction inside at P = 0.50 + 0.30 = 0.80

    The shader calls the estimated fraction inside coverage. At P the close-up shows 80% inside and 1.00 − 0.80 = 0.20, or 20% outside. Step 6 uses these fractions to mix 80% glass color with 20% page color for the whole pixel.

    Use signed distance in code. Dividing by the width tells us how many pixel widths the center is from the outline. Step 1 gave P a minus sign for being inside. The code subtracts the signed fraction so a negative distance adds glass and a positive distance takes glass away.

    P’s signed distance in pixel widths = −0.10 pt ÷ (⅓ pt) = −0.30
    Fraction before limiting = 0.50 − (−0.30) = 0.80

    Where the fade ends. The strip reaches half a pixel to either side of its center. Half a pixel is (⅓ pt) ÷ 2 = ⅙ pt. In the last picture, the center dots U and V are each that far from Q.

    • U is at +⅙ pt. The square’s right side just reaches the outline. The entire square lies on the page side, so coverage is 0.
    • V is at −⅙ pt. The square’s left side just reaches the outline. The entire square lies on the glass side, so coverage is 1.

    Keep coverage between 0 and 1. Slide the center a whole pixel width to either side of Q and the strip stays entirely on that side. The formula can keep going past 0 and 1, so the shader passes its result to clamp(value, 0, 1).

    One width outside = +⅓ pt ÷ (⅓ pt) = +1
    Before limiting = 0.50 − 1 = −0.50
    Coverage = clamp(−0.50, 0, 1) = 0

    One width inside = −⅓ pt ÷ (⅓ pt) = −1
    Before limiting = 0.50 − (−1) = 1.50
    Coverage = clamp(1.50, 0, 1) = 1

    At P the formula gives 0.80. That value already describes a fraction between none and all, so clamp keeps it.

    Pick the menu’s starting color

    A pixel color has three numbers, one for red, one for green, and one for blue. Each number is called a channel. The shader uses 0 for none of that color and 1 for the full amount. Give all three channels the same number and you get gray. Three zeros give black, and three ones give white.

    The tint is the menu’s starting color. In the dark-mode Safari recording, a patch in the middle of the menu measures about 25 on the recording’s scale, where 0 is black and 255 is white. Divide by 255 to express that dark gray on the shader’s scale.

    Recorded gray on the shader’s scale = 25 ÷ 255 ≈ 0.098

    Use the nearby value 0.10 for red, green, and blue. How much could we add to each channel before it reaches 1? Subtract the starting gray from the maximum.

    Room to add per channel = 1.00 − 0.10 = 0.90

    Step 4 works out how much to add at P to draw the rim line.

    Find which way the edge faces

    outwardAt takes P and the menu shape. It reads signed distance half a point each way along x and y. The positions within each pair are 1.00 pt apart because 0.50 + 0.50 = 1.00. Each sample uses the same distance from K minus the radius that we used at P.

    Subtract left from right and top from bottom, then divide each change by the full gap. These changes form an arrow toward increasing signed distance, which is outward. Divide both parts by the arrow’s length to keep only its direction. If both changes are zero, the helper returns a zero arrow.

    Add the bright rim line

    The rim line is the narrow streak just inside the menu edge. The shader draws it by adding the same amount to red, green, and blue near the outline. Equal additions move a gray toward white. We’ll work out that amount at P.

    addRimLine takes the starting gray, P’s signed distance, and the outward direction. For this comparison, the line fades over 2.00 pt just inside the outline. Pixels on the outline get the full share of the line. Pixels 2.00 pt inside get none. smoothstep rounds off both ends of the fade. It multiplies the fraction squared by a curve factor. The factor is 3 minus twice the fraction. The helper skips the line when signed distance is positive.

    The outward direction estimates how the glass faces at the outline near P. That arrow lies flat on the screen, with height part 0. The fitted lamp arrow (−0.48, −0.60, 0.64) points left, up the screen, and out toward you. Multiply matching parts and add them to get the alignment. Use its size so the opposite corner can light up too.

    The chosen all-around light gives every part of the line 0.40. On this scale, 0 means no light and 1 means full light. The 0.40 keeps the dim corners visible. That leaves 1.00 − 0.40 = 0.60 for the lamp. The comparison uses a strength of 0.25, the amount added to each channel at full band and full light. Multiply by the band to set how much line reaches P, and by the light to set how bright it is there.

    P’s addition fits within the 0.90 of room from Step 2. The shader limits each channel to the range from 0 to 1. At P it keeps the gray we just calculated.

    0.10 starting gray ≈ 0.314 after adding the rim line

    Shade the page underneath P

    To draw the shadow 4 pt lower, ask how much mask there was 4 pt above P. The arrow starts at the pixel we color and ends at source S. Positive y goes down, so the read subtracts the offset. A larger offset moves the shadow farther below the menu.

    S.x = P.x = 122.76 pt
    S.y = 655.88 − 4 = 651.88 pt

    Use the same distance rule at S. Now divide by the shadow’s 12 pt fade width. The mask fades from full at 6 pt inside to zero at 6 pt outside. A larger width spreads the transition over more screen points.

    Multiply the mask by the maximum shadow amount. We choose 0.24 so the shadow is easy to see on a flat page. A larger amount makes the shadow darker. The 12 pt width and 4 pt offset are course starting values. The shadow is indistinguishable in the dark recording.

    We choose a page gray of 0.16 so the dark menu and its shadow stand out. A black shadow takes away part of the page color. Multiply the starting page gray by the fraction that remains.

    Combine the two shares at P

    Use the glass gray from Step 4, after adding the rim line. Multiply it by P’s 0.80 coverage from Step 2. Multiply the page gray from Step 5 by the remaining 0.20. Add the two contributions to get one gray for the whole pixel.

    At full coverage, the page contributes zero. Beyond the soft edge, the glass contributes zero and the shaded page can still show the shadow. In the full view, look for two brighter opposite corners and the wider shadow that extends below the menu.

    Inputs the caller gives you

    restingMenuColor receives P in points counted from the menu’s center. Positive x goes right and positive y goes down. The caller supplies halfSize, which is (130.5, 94) pt, and radius, the 36 pt corner radius.

    pixelPt is one device pixel’s width in points. tint and page are the starting colors. shadowOffsetPt, shadowWidthPt, and shadowStrength tell you how far to shift the shadow, how wide its fade is, and how much it darkens the page.

    RestingMenuHelpers.h already provides roundedRectSd, outwardAt, and addRimLine. Here’s what to pass to each geometry call.

    What to findCallWhere the point is measured from
    Signed distance at ProundedRectSd(P, halfSize, radius)P is measured from the menu’s center.
    Signed distance for the shadowroundedRectSd(S, halfSize, radius)For the shadow read S, subtract shadowOffsetPt from P’s y. S is also measured from the menu’s center.
    Outward direction at PoutwardAt(P, halfSize, radius, 0.5)P is measured from the menu’s center. The helper samples 0.5 pt to either side of P.

    How the shader builds the menu

    1. Find signed distance at P. Use the one-device-pixel width pixelPt to get the glass fraction from Step 2.
    2. Find signed distance at S. Use the supplied shadowWidthPt and shadowStrength to darken the page as in Step 5.
    3. Find the outward direction at P. Give addRimLine the starting gray tint, the signed distance at P, and that direction. Limit each resulting channel to 0 through 1.
    4. Mix that glass color with the darkened page using the two fractions from Step 6. Return the mixed color.
    How the prepared caller centers the menu

    SwiftUI’s colorEffect gives the shader position and the incoming pixel color. The first entry in Swift’s render list, .boundingRect, supplies the preview’s top-left position and size. The caller centers the menu in the preview and subtracts that center from position to get P. It also halves the menu size that Swift supplies.

    Center x = 300 ÷ 2 = 150 pt
    Center y = 226 ÷ 2 = 113 pt
    Menu half-width = 261 ÷ 2 = 130.5 pt
    Menu half-height = 188 ÷ 2 = 94 pt

    The worked example in Section 1 places the menu on a full phone screen. To use the same spot on the menu in the preview, keep the same offset from its center. The offset tells you how far left and above the center P is.

    P’s x offset from the menu center = 122.76 − 238.8 = −116.04 pt
    P’s y offset from the menu center = 655.88 − 742.6 = −86.72 pt
    P’s x in the preview = 150 − 116.04 = 33.96 pt
    P’s y in the preview = 113 − 86.72 = 26.28 pt
    Read the completed shader line by line

    This is the function from RestingMenuExercise.metal. half3 holds red, green, and blue in the GPU’s short number format. The finished pixel already includes the page color, so the caller returns full opacity.

    static half3 restingMenuColor(
                       float2 P,
                       float radius,
                       float pixelPt,
                       half3 tint,
                       half3 page,
                       float shadowOffsetPt,
                       float shadowWidthPt,
                       float shadowStrength,
                       float2 halfSize) {
    float sd = roundedRectSd(P, halfSize, radius);
    float fractionGained = sd / pixelPt;
    float fractionInside = clamp(0.5 - fractionGained, 0.0, 1.0);
    float fractionOutside = 1 - fractionInside;

    float2 outward = outwardAt(P, halfSize, radius, 0.5);

    float3 rimGray = clamp(float3(addRimLine(tint, sd, outward)), 0.0, 1.0);

    float shiftedSd = roundedRectSd(P + float2(0, -shadowOffsetPt), halfSize, radius);
    float distanceFadeWidths = shiftedSd / shadowWidthPt;
    float shadowMask = clamp(0.5 - distanceFadeWidths, 0.0, 1.0);
    float shadowAmount = shadowStrength * shadowMask;
    float3 pageGray = float3(page) * (1.0 - shadowAmount);

    return half3(float3(rimGray) * fractionInside + pageGray * fractionOutside);
}
