Lesson 11.2 — The push: largest on the outline, 0 on the flat top.

Look at the blue stripe beside the amber mark. It bends near the menu’s edge. In Lesson 11.1, an output pixel stayed in place and took its color from another point in the glass picture. Here you’ll use the shape of the rim to work out how far one pixel reads and which point gives it its color.

Imagine sliding the whole picture by the same amount. The stripe would stay straight. Here each row near the edge reads a different distance inward. Those rows show pieces of the stripe from different places, so the pieces no longer line up along a straight line. The distance a pixel reads is the push.

Where this is used. Lesson 11.3 compares the source points for neighboring pixels to explain the upside-down band in flight. The lens build in Lesson 11.4 reads those source points in the glass picture so you can see both the stripes and the menu rows bend.

Uses

  • The glass picture contains the blurred page, tint, and menu rows. The sample supplies a color for the output pixel. Lesson 11.1
  • Signed distance is zero on the outline and negative inside. outwardAt gives an arrow pointing out, scaled to length 1 wherever the samples give a direction. Lesson 10.46
  • In a triangle with a square corner, the two short sides squared and added give the longest side squared. Lesson 10.1

Section 1 · follow one pixelWhere does P read?

Start at the upper-left corner of the resting menu. On the screen, positions are in points (pt) from the top-left of the 402 × 874 pt screen. x increases to the right and y increases toward the bottom. The drawings enlarge a small part of that screen. Green marks values we have before a calculation.

    Find the slice through P

    P is the output pixel. Use the same chosen pixel as in the outward-arrow lesson. Its center is at (126.06, 660.28) pt. The rim is the curved edge of the glass. Q is the nearest point on the outline, at the foot of that curve. The rounded outline comes from the resting Safari frame. The signed-distance helper gives −5.6 pt at P. The minus says P is inside, so the distance from Q to P is 5.6 pt.

    The purple line in the screen view goes from Q through P. In the turnable model, the purple sheet stands up along that line. It cuts the rim through P so we can look straight at the cut from the side.

    Find the height over P

    In the side view, start at Q and count right toward the inside of the glass. Height starts at the screen and increases upward. The model uses a quarter circle for this cut. We choose a resting rim 14 pt wide to keep the bending close to the edge, as the recording shows when the menu settles. The circle’s radius is also 14 pt, so the flat top starts 14 pt from Q and is 14 pt high.

    K is the circle’s center on the screen, directly below the start of the flat top. H is the point where the curve sits directly above P. The line from K to H is a radius. The line from P to K runs along the screen and meets the height from P to H at a square corner. To get the height squared, take the radius squared and subtract the horizontal leg squared. The ² means multiply a length by itself.

    The square root finds the positive height whose square is 125.44. H is 11.2 pt above P.

    Turn the height into a read distance

    At the resting outline, the model reads 6 pt inward. The course chooses this small maximum push to match the faint bending as the menu settles. Between the outline and the flat top, the model scales those 6 pt by the share of height still left to climb. The full height is 14 pt. We found 11.2 pt over P in the previous step.

    The share runs from 1 at the bottom of the rim to 0 at the flat top. At P, 0.2 means one fifth of the largest read.

    Read farther inside

    At P, the direction helper gives approximately (−0.60, −0.80), shown by the amber arrow. It checks how signed distance changes for a small move to the right and a small move down. At P, both changes are negative, so the arrow points the other way. The helper scales the arrow to length 1. The drawn amber arrow is longer so you can see it.

    The teal pixel P needs the color from S, the source point 1.2 pt farther inside. Multiply each part of the outward arrow by the push from the previous step. Subtract those parts from P to go the other way. Subtracting a negative adds to that position.

    The green read arrow starts at P and ends at S. layer.sample(S) takes the color from S and returns it for P. P stays at (126.06, 660.28) pt.

    Check the outline and flat top

    At Q the rim has 0 pt of height. At K the rim reaches the full 14 pt. Compare their read distances with the read at P. Each arrow starts on the screen and points inward.

    Farther inside, the height stays at 14 pt. Every pixel there keeps reading at its own place.

    Compare the read in flight

    Keep the same 5.6 pt from Q to P. This comparison holds the slice in place so we can see what the settings do. During flight, the model uses a 28 pt rim and a 24 pt maximum read on the outline. The course chooses these larger settings to match the wider, stronger bending during the opening. The quarter circle is 28 pt high at its flat top.

    P has reached a smaller share of the taller rim. That leaves more of a larger maximum read. The arrow grows from 1.2 pt to 9.6 pt.

    Where the rim settings came from

    The course fits this model by eye to the dark-mode Safari opening on an iPhone 16 Pro. At about 0.252 s after the tap, text bends along the outline. By 0.291 s the visible bending is down to roughly a 10–15 pt strip. The rows look straight at rest. The course chooses a 14 pt rim and a 6 pt maximum read for the small resting effect, and a 28 pt rim and a 24 pt maximum read for the wider, stronger effect in flight. A wider rim carries the effect farther into the glass. A larger maximum read makes the pixel look farther inward at the same share of height.

    The recording shows the picture bending. The quarter circle and the rule that scales the read by the height left to climb are choices for the reconstruction. The side profile is hidden in the recording, so there is no measured height to compare with the drawn profile. Safari menu plan, refraction layer

    Section 4 · Metal for the lens buildCalculate the source point and read its color

    The function takes position and the glass picture from Lesson 11.1. The caller supplies the resting menu’s center, its half-size and corner radius, and the two rim settings. The settings are positive lengths in points. Pass position and menuCenter in the same coordinates. The existing geometry helpers take a position counted from the menu center. The sample still uses the layer’s coordinates.

    For the table, the screen and layer share an origin. The existing resting menu settings are center (238.8, 742.6) pt, half-size (130.5, 94) pt, and corner radius 36 pt. They come from the outline fitted to the resting frame. Use the earlier helper’s 0.5 pt sample step on each side of P. The caller from 11.1 allows a read of up to 24 pt along each axis.

    #include <metal_stdlib>
#include <SwiftUI/SwiftUI.h>
#include "RestingMenuHelpers.h"
using namespace metal;

[[ stitchable ]] half4 readRim(
    float2 position, SwiftUI::Layer layer, float2 menuCenter,
    float2 halfSize, float radius, float bevel, float edgePush
) {
    float2 p = position - menuCenter;
    float sd = restingMenu::roundedRectSd(p, halfSize, radius);
    if (sd > 0.0) return layer.sample(position);
    float depth = -sd;
    if (depth >= bevel) return layer.sample(position);
    float leg = bevel - depth;
    float h = sqrt(bevel * bevel - leg * leg);
    float push = edgePush * (1.0 - h / bevel);
    float2 outward = restingMenu::outwardAt(p, halfSize, radius, 0.5);
    float2 source = position - outward * push;
    return layer.sample(source);
}
    LineWhat it does at P
    p = …Subtracts the center’s two positions from (126.06, 660.28) pt. P is (−112.74, −82.32) pt from the menu center.
    sd = …The distance helper gives approximately −5.6 pt. Outside the outline, the function leaves the layer’s sample at its original position.
    depth = …Turns the signed distance into 5.6 pt inside. At or beyond the rim width the read stays at the pixel itself.
    leg and hThe line from P to K is 8.4 pt. The square root gives 11.2 pt over P.
    push = …One fifth of the 6 pt maximum is 1.2 pt.
    outward = …The earlier helper gives approximately (−0.60, −0.80). If the samples give no direction, the helper returns (0, 0) and the read stays at the pixel.
    source = …Subtracts the outward move to read inward, at about (126.78, 661.24) pt.
    return layer.sample(source)Returns the sampled color for P. The lens build applies the outline coverage to the result.
