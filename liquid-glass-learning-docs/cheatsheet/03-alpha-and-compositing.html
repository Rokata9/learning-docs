Alpha and compositing

Lessons 1.6 · 3.3 – 3.5 · 3.8 · 3.10 · 3.11 · 4.7 – 4.9 · 7.5

Source = the upper color, drawn now. Destination = the color already at the same position underneath. Every formula here reads one position only: no neighbour, so no blur and no bending. The color chips on each card show what is drawn over what; a checkerboard behind a chip is its transparent part.

Alpha mixture over an opaque background

1.61.7
result = F \times A + B \times (1 - A)
Fone channel of the foreground (the panel drawn on top), 0 … 1. Bthe same channel of the opaque background at the same position. Athe foreground's alpha: 0 shows only B, 1 shows only F.
+=
Example the picture: a white panel over dark gray (all three channels alike)
F = 1.00 whiteA = 0.25B = 0.20 dark gray
\begin{aligned} result &= F \times A + B \times (1 - A) \\ &= \kn{1.00} \times \kn{0.25} + \kn{0.20} \times (1 - \kn{0.25}) \\ &= 0.25 + 0.20 \times (1 - 0.25) \\ &= 0.25 + 0.20 \times 0.75 \\ &= 0.25 + 0.15 = 0.40 \end{aligned}

Source-over, full RGBA (straight)

1.63.33.4
\begin{aligned} w_D &= A_D \times (1 - A_S) \\ A_{out} &= A_S + w_D \\ C_{out} &= (C_S \times A_S + C_D \times w_D) \div A_{out} \end{aligned}
C_S, A_Sone channel and the alpha of the source S, drawn now on top. C_D, A_Dthe same channel and the alpha of the destination D, already there. w_Dhow much of D still shows through S. Work out the alphas first. A_{out}, C_{out}the result's alpha and channel. With an opaque D (A_D = 1) this is the alpha mixture card.

Premultiplied RGBA

3.54.8
\begin{aligned} P &= C \times A \\ P_{out} &= P_S + P_D \times (1 - A_S) \\ A_{out} &= A_S + A_D \times (1 - A_S) \\ C_{out} &= P_{out} \div A_{out} \quad \text{(only if } A_{out} > 0\text{)} \end{aligned}
C, Aa straight channel and its alpha, as in source-over. Pthe premultiplied channel: the channel already multiplied by its alpha. P_S, P_Dthe premultiplied channel of the source and of the destination. C_{out}the result as a straight channel again.
Example the same S over D as the source-over card
A_S = 0.50,\; A_D = 0.601 - A_S = 0.50,\; A_{out} = 0.80 from the source-over card
channelP_S = C_S \times A_SP_D = C_D \times A_DP_D \times (1 - A_S), then P_{out}C_{out}
red0.90 × 0.50 = 0.450.10 × 0.60 = 0.060.06 × 0.50 = 0.03
0.45 + 0.03 = 0.48		0.48 ÷ 0.80 = 0.60
green0.10 × 0.50 = 0.050.90 × 0.60 = 0.540.54 × 0.50 = 0.27
0.05 + 0.27 = 0.32		0.32 ÷ 0.80 = 0.40
blue0.80 × 0.50 = 0.400.40 × 0.60 = 0.240.24 × 0.50 = 0.12
0.40 + 0.12 = 0.52		0.52 ÷ 0.80 = 0.65
Which form? SwiftUI Color(red:green:blue:opacity:) takes straight values. A premultiplied buffer (for example CGImageAlphaInfo.premultipliedLast) stores premultiplied values. A bare RGB triple does not say which; label it.

Tint

3.87.5
\begin{aligned} output &= tint \times amount + base \times (1 - amount) \\ &= base + amount \times (tint - base) \end{aligned}
baseone channel of the opaque color underneath. tintthe same channel of the tint color drawn over it. amountthe tint's alpha, 0 … 1: 0 keeps the base, 1 gives the tint color. This is source-over with an opaque base. tint - baseits sign says the direction: plus, the channel rises toward the tint; minus, it falls.

Blend modes (opaque layers)

3.10
\begin{aligned} \text{Normal:}\quad out &= s \\ \text{Multiply:}\quad out &= s \times d \\ \text{Screen:}\quad out &= 1 - (1 - s) \times (1 - d) \\ \text{Plus:}\quad out &= clamp(s + d,\; 0,\; 1) \end{aligned}
sone channel of the source (the upper layer). dthe same channel of the destination (the lower layer). neutrala source that changes nothing: white (1) for Multiply, black (0) for Screen and Plus.
→
Example the two colors of the blend-mode diagram
s = (0.84, 0.36, 0.18)d = (0.22, 0.58, 0.76)
channelMultiply s \times dScreen 1 - (1 - s) \times (1 - d)Plus clamp(s + d)

1 − 0.1248 = 0.8752		0.84 + 0.22 = 1.06 → 1
green0.36 × 0.58 = 0.20880.64 × 0.42 = 0.2688
1 − 0.2688 = 0.7312		0.36 + 0.58 = 0.94

The 1 − s and 1 − d values: red 1 − 0.84 = 0.16 and 1 − 0.22 = 0.78; green 0.64 and 0.42; blue 0.82 and 0.24.
With alpha (Multiply, general form): out = s × (1 − A_D) + d × (1 − A_S) + s × d. With both alphas 1 it is s × d.

Stack of layers

3.11
\begin{aligned} running &= base \\ \text{for each layer:}\quad running &= sourceOver(layer,\; running) \end{aligned}
basethe bottom color; opaque here, so every running result stays opaque (alpha 1). runningthe result so far: the destination for the next layer. layereach layer in draw order, with its own alpha A_L.
+=+=
Example two layers over an opaque blue base
base = (0.22, 0.58, 0.76)A: (0.90, 0.30, 0.20),\; A_L = 0.50B: (0.20, 0.20, 0.60),\; A_L = 0.25
layer, channellayer \times A_Lrunning \times (1 - A_L)new running
A, red0.90 × 0.50 = 0.450.22 × 0.50 = 0.110.45 + 0.11 = 0.56
A, green0.30 × 0.50 = 0.150.58 × 0.50 = 0.290.15 + 0.29 = 0.44
A, blue0.20 × 0.50 = 0.100.76 × 0.50 = 0.380.10 + 0.38 = 0.48
B, red0.20 × 0.25 = 0.050.56 × 0.75 = 0.420.05 + 0.42 = 0.47
B, green0.20 × 0.25 = 0.050.44 × 0.75 = 0.330.05 + 0.33 = 0.38
B, blue0.60 × 0.25 = 0.150.48 × 0.75 = 0.360.15 + 0.36 = 0.51
1 − A_L: 1 − 0.50 = 0.50 for A, 1 − 0.25 = 0.75 for B. Layer B uses A's result as its destination.

Mask (destinationIn)

4.84.7
\begin{aligned} P_{masked} &= P_{content} \times mask \\ result &= sourceOver(masked,\; parent) \end{aligned}
P_{content}the content's premultiplied RGBA at one position (x, y). maskthe mask's alpha at the same position, 0 … 1. parentwhat is already drawn underneath, here opaque.

Coverage and antialiasing

4.9
\begin{aligned} coverage &= insideProbes \div allProbes \\ edgeAlpha &= shapeAlpha \times coverage \end{aligned}
insideProbesthe test points inside the pixel square whose center is inside the shape. allProbesall test points of the pixel, such as 12 × 12 = 144. coveragethe fraction of the pixel's area inside the shape, 0 … 1. shapeAlphathe shape's own alpha; edgeAlpha is the alpha the edge pixel is drawn with.
Example the coverage diagram at its start values
allProbes = 12 \times 12insideProbes = 94shapeAlpha = 0.50
\begin{aligned} allProbes &= \kn{12} \times \kn{12} = 144 \\ coverage &= insideProbes \div allProbes = \kn{94} \div 144 = 0.653 \\ edgeAlpha &= shapeAlpha \times coverage = \kn{0.50} \times 0.653 = 0.327 \end{aligned}
Few probes are coarse: with 2 × 2 probes a pixel whose true covered area is 40% can report 2 ÷ 4 = 50%.
