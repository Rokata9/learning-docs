Ch 3

Color, opacity, and compositing

Lessons 3.1 – 3.11

Alpha and compositing. Source = the upper color, drawn now. Destination = the color already at the same position underneath. Every formula here reads one position only: no neighbour, so no blur and no bending. The color chips on each card show what is drawn over what; a checkerboard behind a chip is its transparent part.

Cards on other chapter pages that this chapter's lessons use
  • Source-over, full RGBA (straight): on the Chapter 1 page; uses Lessons 3.3, 3.4 of this chapter.

Channels: 0 … 1 and 8-bit

3.13.28.3
\begin{aligned} channel8 &= round(channel \times 255) \\ channel &= channel8 \div 255 \\ alpha &= percent \div 100 \end{aligned}
channelone of red, green, blue or alpha, from 0 to 1. RGBA(red, green, blue, alpha) is one color. channel8the same channel stored in 8 bits: a whole number from 0 to 255. percentan opacity written in percent, such as 75%.
Example the teaching orange
(red, green, blue) = (0.88, 0.48, 0.18)percent = 75
\begin{aligned} red8 &= round(\kn{0.88} \times 255) = round(224.4) = 224 \\ green8 &= round(\kn{0.48} \times 255) = round(122.4) = 122 \\ blue8 &= round(\kn{0.18} \times 255) = round(45.9) = 46 \\ red &= red8 \div 255 = 224 \div 255 = 0.878 \\ alpha &= \kn{75} \div 100 = 0.75 \end{aligned}
Note: 0.878 is not 0.88 again: the 8-bit store keeps only 256 steps. 0.40 alone is one channel, not a color; RGB(0.40, 0.40, 0.40) with alpha 1 is gray #666666.

Premultiplied RGBA

3.54.8
\begin{aligned} P &= C \times A \\ P_{out} &= P_S + P_D \times (1 - A_S) \\ A_{out} &= A_S + A_D \times (1 - A_S) \\ C_{out} &= P_{out} \div A_{out} \quad \text{(only if } A_{out} > 0\text{)} \end{aligned}
C, Aa straight channel and its alpha, as in source-over. Pthe premultiplied channel: the channel already multiplied by its alpha. P_S, P_Dthe premultiplied channel of the source and of the destination. C_{out}the result as a straight channel again.

From a stored color to a contrast ratio

3.63.77.67.12
\begin{aligned} \text{1. decode each channel:}\quad linear &= \begin{cases} s \div 12.92 & \text{if } s \le 0.04045 \\ ((s + 0.055) \div 1.055)^{2.4} & \text{if } s > 0.04045 \end{cases} \\ \text{2. relative luminance:}\quad L &= 0.2126 \times R_{lin} + 0.7152 \times G_{lin} + 0.0722 \times B_{lin} \\ \text{3. contrast ratio:}\quad ratio &= (L_{lighter} + 0.05) \div (L_{darker} + 0.05) \\ \text{4. text color:}\quad & \text{the one with the larger ratio; it passes if } ratio \ge 4.5 \end{aligned}
sa stored sRGB channel, 0 … 1: the number in Color(red:green:blue:). linearthe same channel as an amount of light. The small 2.4 is a power, not × 2.4. R_{lin}, G_{lin}, B_{lin}the three decoded channels. Each feeds step 2 with its own weight; green counts most. Lrelative luminance, 0 (black) … 1 (white). Alpha is not in it: alpha acts earlier, in source-over. L_{lighter}, L_{darker}the L of the lighter and of the darker of the two colors. 0.05 is a fixed constant of the WCAG standard. 4.5the WCAG minimum for normal text: a policy step, checked after the ratio.
Example the course color as background
(R, G, B) = (0.80, 0.25, 0.60) stored sRGBblack: L = 0white: L = 1
channelss + 0.055÷ 1.055(\ldots)^{2.4} = linearweightweight × linear
red0.800.80 + 0.055 = 0.8550.855 ÷ 1.055 = 0.810.812.4 = 0.6030.21260.2126 × 0.603 = 0.128
green0.250.25 + 0.055 = 0.3050.305 ÷ 1.055 = 0.2890.2892.4 = 0.0510.71520.7152 × 0.051 = 0.036
blue0.600.60 + 0.055 = 0.6550.655 ÷ 1.055 = 0.6210.6212.4 = 0.3190.07220.0722 × 0.319 = 0.023
\begin{aligned} L &= 0.128 + 0.036 + 0.023 = 0.164 + 0.023 = 0.187 \\ ratio_{black} &= (L + 0.05) \div (\kn{0} + 0.05) = (0.187 + 0.05) \div 0.05 = 0.237 \div 0.05 = 4.74 \\ ratio_{white} &= (\kn{1} + 0.05) \div (L + 0.05) = 1.05 \div (0.187 + 0.05) = 1.05 \div 0.237 = 4.43 \\ \text{choice} &: 4.74 > 4.43 \to \text{black text,}\; 4.74 \ge 4.5 \to \text{it passes} \end{aligned}
Notes: all channels here are above 0.04045, so every one takes the power line; a dark channel such as s = 0.02 takes the other line: 0.02 ÷ 12.92 = 0.002. Lessons 3.6 and 3.7 keep 6 decimals (L = 0.187759) and print 4.76 and 4.42: the same choice. The ratio runs from 1 : 1 (two equal colors) to 21 : 1 (black on white: 1.05 ÷ 0.05 = 21).

3.87.5
\begin{aligned} output &= tint \times amount + base \times (1 - amount) \\ &= base + amount \times (tint - base) \end{aligned}
baseone channel of the opaque color underneath. tintthe same channel of the tint color drawn over it. amountthe tint's alpha, 0 … 1: 0 keeps the base, 1 gives the tint color. Color matrix (4 × 5)

3.97.6
\begin{bmatrix} R' \\ G' \\ B' \\ A' \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} m_{00} & m_{01} & m_{02} & m_{03} & m_{04} \\ m_{10} & m_{11} & m_{12} & m_{13} & m_{14} \\ m_{20} & m_{21} & m_{22} & m_{23} & m_{24} \\ m_{30} & m_{31} & m_{32} & m_{33} & m_{34} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} R \\ G \\ B \\ A \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}
R' = m_{00} \times R + m_{01} \times G + m_{02} \times B + m_{03} \times A + m_{04} \times 1
R, G, B, Athe input color, channels 0 … 1. R', G', B', A'the output color: each output channel is one row of the matrix times the five inputs, added up. m_{04}, m_{14}, …the fifth column multiplies a constant 1, so it is an offset added to the channel.
The saturation matrix of the lab, for a saturation factor s (the frozen frost uses s = 0.40):
\begin{bmatrix} 0.213(1-s)+s & 0.715(1-s) & 0.072(1-s) & 0 & 0 \\ 0.213(1-s) & 0.715(1-s)+s & 0.072(1-s) & 0 & 0 \\ 0.213(1-s) & 0.715(1-s) & 0.072(1-s)+s & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}

Saturation (lab matrix)

3.97.6
gray = 0.213 × R + 0.715 × G + 0.072 × B ← weights add to 1.000
output = gray + factor × (channel − gray)
shown = clamp(output, 0, 1) ← alpha is copied unchanged
factor < 1 moves each channel toward gray, 1 keeps it, > 1 pushes it away. The gray reference is not relative luminance (different weights, no sRGB decode). Frozen frost factor: 0.40.
same thing as the 4 × 5 matrix row for red (s = factor):
( 0.213 × (1 − s) + s, 0.715 × (1 − s), 0.072 × (1 − s), 0, 0 )

Blend modes (opaque layers)

3.10
\begin{aligned} \text{Normal:}\quad out &= s \\ \text{Multiply:}\quad out &= s \times d \\ \text{Screen:}\quad out &= 1 - (1 - s) \times (1 - d) \\ \text{Plus:}\quad out &= clamp(s + d,\; 0,\; 1) \end{aligned}
sone channel of the source (the upper layer). dthe same channel of the destination (the lower layer). neutrala source that changes nothing: white (1) for Multiply, black (0) for Screen and Plus.

Stack of layers

basethe bottom color; opaque here, so every running result stays opaque (alpha 1). runningthe result so far: the destination for the next layer. layereach layer in draw order, with its own alpha A_L.
