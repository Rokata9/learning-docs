Points and pixels

2.58.48.5
\begin{aligned} px &= pt \times displayScale \\ pt &= px \div displayScale \\ fragCoord &= position \times displayScale \\ resolution &= (widthPt \times displayScale,\; heightPt \times displayScale) \end{aligned}
pta SwiftUI layout point. Layout, Canvas, .offset and .blur(radius:) use points. pxa device pixel. Shader math uses pixels. displayScalepixels per point: 3 on the iPhone 16 Pro. positiona point of the view, in pt from its top-left corner. fragCoordthe same point in px: what the shader receives. resolutionthe view's width and height in px.
Example a view 96 pt wide on the iPhone 16 Pro
widthPt = 96 the view's widthdisplayScale = 3 iPhone 16 Proposition = (2, 1) point P in the picture, pt
\begin{aligned} px &= pt \times displayScale = \kn{96} \times \kn{3} = 288 \\ pt &= px \div displayScale = 288 \div \kn{3} = 96 \\ fragCoord &= position \times displayScale = (\kn{2} \times \kn{3},\; \kn{1} \times \kn{3}) = (6, 3) \end{aligned}
Lesson 2.5 teaches with a made-up scale of 2.75 px per pt, so the two units cannot be confused: 96 × 2.75 = 264 px.

Local, parent, window, screen

2.62.72.9
\begin{aligned} P_{parent} &= O_{control} + P_{local} \\ P_{local} &= P_{parent} - O_{control} \\ P_{screen} &= O_{window} + O_{parent} + O_{control} + P_{local} \end{aligned}
P_{local}point P counted from the control's own top-left corner. O_{control}the control's top-left corner, counted in its parent. O_{parent}the parent's top-left corner, counted in the window. O_{window}the window's top-left corner, counted on the screen. +, −pairs add and subtract member by member: x with x, y with y.
Example point P of the picture, all in pt
P_{local} = (40, 30)O_{control} = (150, 100)O_{parent} = (60, 200)O_{window} = (20, 40)
\begin{aligned} P_{parent} &= O_{control} + P_{local} = (\kn{150} + \kn{40},\; \kn{100} + \kn{30}) = (190, 130) \\ P_{window} &= O_{parent} + P_{parent} = (\kn{60} + 190,\; \kn{200} + 130) = (250, 330) \\ P_{screen} &= O_{window} + P_{window} = (\kn{20} + 250,\; \kn{40} + 330) = (270, 370) \\ P_{local} &= P_{parent} - O_{control} = (190 - \kn{150},\; 130 - \kn{100}) = (40, 30) \end{aligned}
Note: always say which space a point belongs to (“P = (40, 30) pt, local to the control”).

Normalized coordinates

2.82.9
\begin{aligned} normalizedX &= localX \div width &\qquad localX &= normalizedX \times width \\ normalizedY &= localY \div height &\qquad localY &= normalizedY \times height \end{aligned}
localX, localYa point in pt from the rectangle's top-left corner, y down. width, heightthe rectangle's size in pt. normalizedX, normalizedYthe same point as fractions of the size: 0 at the left or top edge, 1 at the right or bottom edge.
Example point Q of the picture; the rectangle then grows
Q = (160, 270) ptwidth = 640, height = 360 beforewidth = 800, height = 500 after
\begin{aligned} normalizedX &= localX \div width = \kn{160} \div \kn{640} = 0.25 \\ normalizedY &= localY \div height = \kn{270} \div \kn{360} = 0.75 \\ localX &= normalizedX \times width = 0.25 \times \kn{800} = 200 \\ localY &= normalizedY \times height = 0.75 \times \kn{500} = 375 \end{aligned}
Last pixel: in an 8-px row the last pixel's center is at 7.5 px, so its normalized x is 7.5 ÷ 8 = 0.9375, not 1.
