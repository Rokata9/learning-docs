Lesson 9.11 on one card: what to keep for the next lessons

Lesson 9.11 adds one idea to Lesson 9.10: the push is scaled by a falloff, so the background moves fully inside the circle, less and less across the band, and not at all outside it. The pixel never moves; only the place where it reads the wallpaper moves. Everything else on this page — the curves, the searches, the derivations — explains what you see. The shader itself is the short chain below.

2 — How long the push is

  • Inside the inner ring (d below 0.15): the full push, dispLen = 0.10.
  • Across the band (d from 0.15 to 0.35): the push shrinks with smoothstep. On the circle edge (d = 0.25) it is half: 0.05.
  • Outside the outer ring (d above 0.35): no push. The wallpaper is not moved.
  • Direction: always straight toward C. The read point is closer to C than the pixel by the push.
  • dispLen is a distance in UV, not a percentage. Deleting “× falloff” gives back Lesson 9.10: the same push everywhere.

3 — What you see, and why

  • Straight lines bend near the circle. The white line x = 0.65 appears as the light-green curve: pixels to the right of the line read it, because their push moves the read point left, toward C.
  • Near C the wallpaper looks enlarged. The pixels from 0.10 to 0.15 from C read the small disc within 0.05 of C: a small part of the wallpaper fills a wider ring.
  • The very middle looks turned around. A pixel closer than 0.10 to C has a push longer than its distance, so its read point lies past C, on the other side (the pixel Z).
  • Across the band the wallpaper looks squeezed. The pixels from 0.15 to 0.35 from C read the wallpaper from 0.05 to 0.35: 0.30 of wallpaper in 0.20 of space.
  • Why smoothstep. With “hard if” (full push inside, none outside) a strip of the wallpaper is never read: straight lines break at the circle edge. With “linear”, the spacing of the read points changes all at once at the rings: lines get corners there. Smoothstep has neither.

4 — Keep two problems apart

Where the glass is, and where to read, are separate problems. The mask — sd and falloff — decides how much a pixel's read point moves. The sample coordinates — readUv — decide where the color comes from. The pixel grid itself never moves. This is the checkpoint question of Chapter 9: “Explain why the geometry mask and the background sample coordinates are separate problems.”

What is ours and what is Apple's. Apple does not publish how Liquid Glass works inside. This course builds its own model of the effect: a shape's sd says where the glass is (Lessons 9.2–9.6), a falloff says how strongly the background moves near its edge (9.11), a displaced read bends the background (9.10–9.11), and later lessons add safety, color fringes, and light. The model is chosen to look right; it is not a copy of Apple's code.
