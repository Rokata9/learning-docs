Chapter 1 · Lesson 1.2 · Observation only

Record initial coverage and refinement

Separate two visible facts. First, determine whether the displayed photo rectangle is completely covered by image pixels. Then determine whether higher-detail regions replace coarser regions without moving the intended framing.

No Android changesReal test-image geometryLessons 1.1 and 1.2 remain incomplete

Scope for this lesson

  • Observe: fit-scale coverage and one deep-zoom refinement sequence.
  • Inspect: the generated coordinate grid around one known tile boundary.
  • Do not infer: the exact decoder, scheduler, or pyramid-level-selection algorithm.
  • Keep sealed: every implementation file under tiled-engine/src/.
Where this fits

Lesson 1.1 recorded the broad journey. Lesson 1.2 isolates image quality.

Coverage question

Inside the displayed photo rectangle, does every visible position contain pixels from the focused photo?

A region may be blurry and still provide complete coverage.

Refinement question

After coverage exists, do higher-detail regions appear in the same screen positions?

Sharpness may change. The photo scale, centre, grid alignment, and tile-edge alignment should not jump.

Vocabulary

Five terms for this observation

Displayed photo rectangleThe screen rectangle occupied by the photo after fitting or zooming. Black area outside this rectangle is not missing photo coverage.
CoverageEvery position inside the displayed photo rectangle contains some pixels from the correct focused photo, even when those pixels are not yet the highest useful detail.
RefinementA higher-detail image region replaces or covers a coarser displayed region while preserving the intended scale and position.
Source coordinateA position measured in the generated image's own pixel grid. For example, x = 1024 means 1024 source pixels from the image's left edge.
Tile boundaryThe shared edge between two independently handled rectangular image regions. A correct boundary has no gap, overlap, or coordinate discontinuity.
One real value trace

Use source coordinate x = 1024 as the diagnostic boundary

The activity's grid uses a 256-pixel interval. Its documentation identifies 512-pixel tile boundaries. The value 1024 belongs to both systems.

Diagnostic grid
1024 ÷ 256 = 4

x = 1024 is the fourth 256-pixel grid interval from the left.

Tile boundary
1024 ÷ 512 = 2

x = 1024 is the boundary after two 512-pixel tile columns.

Neighboring source regions
512…1023 | 1024…1535

The left region ends at source pixel 1023. The next region begins at 1024.

Right edge
3008 = 5 × 512 + 448

Five full-width regions leave a final 448-pixel edge region.

What consumes this value

During the deep-zoom check, the black grid line at x = 1024 is the visible marker. Both neighboring regions must place that same source boundary at the same screen position.

A gap, double line, shifted label, or hue discontinuity is an observable defect. The observation alone does not identify the faulty internal function.

    Do not touch the screen for about two seconds. Observe holes inside the photo rectangle, visible sharpening, and any scale or centre jump.

    Exercises

    Four diagnostic checks

    Exercise 1 — The landscape photo has black bars above and below it at fit scale. Is fit-scale coverage incomplete?

    Answer: No. Coverage is judged only inside the displayed photo rectangle. The black bars are outside the fitted photo because the photo and portrait viewport have different aspect ratios.

    Exercise 2 — The full-resolution width is 3008 pixels and the intended tile width is 512 pixels. How wide is the final right-edge tile?

    Answer: 448 pixels. Five full tiles cover 5 × 512 = 2560 pixels. The remaining width is 3008 − 2560 = 448.

    Exercise 3 — A region becomes sharper, but the x = 1024 line shifts three screen pixels at that moment. Is that normal refinement?

    Answer: No. Refinement may change detail, but the same source boundary should remain at the same screen position when the transform is unchanged. The observation proves a visible continuity or placement defect. It does not yet prove which internal layer caused it.

    Exercise 4 — The recording shows the first photo already sharp. Does that prove the engine never used coarser coverage?

    Answer: No. Coarser work may have completed before the first presented recording frame, higher-detail content may already have been available, or recording compression may hide the change. A trace is required to identify the internal level sequence.
