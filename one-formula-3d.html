Why x ÷ z and y ÷ z turn a 3D point into a point on a flat screen

A companion to tsoding's video “One Formula That Demystifies 3D Graphics” and its code, github.com/tsoding/formula. Every picture on this page is live: you can turn it and click its dots.

What the code draws

The code's canvas: 800 × 800 pixels, counted from its top-left corner. In this top picture the cube is pushed 1.5 units forward (the code uses 1), so the cube and its drawing on the screen do not overlap.

x′ = x ÷ z
y′ = y ÷ z

A 3D point is three numbers (x, y, z): how far the point is to the right of your eye, above your eye, and in front of your eye. To draw the point, the code divides the first two numbers by the third. The result (x′, y′) (read “x prime, y prime”) is where the point appears on the screen.

Left picture: a cube in front of the eye E. Each pink line runs from a cube corner to E. Where a pink line crosses the blue screen, the code draws that corner. Right picture: the same drawing on the code's canvas.

How to use the 3D pictures: drag to turn the picture · Shift + drag (or right-button drag) to move it · Ctrl + scroll, or pinch on a trackpad, to zoom · double-click to reset · click any dot to see its coordinates. The buttons in each picture's top-left corner jump to a fixed view.

2 · From the screen to canvas pixels: screen()

project() gives x′ and y′ between −1 and 1 (the picture starts with P′ = (0.500, 0.300) from Section 1). The canvas needs pixels: 0 to 800, counted from its top-left corner. The function screen() converts one into the other. In every step the point stays where it is; only the ruler we measure it with changes. Drag the amber point P′.


      

    

  






  
3 · A cube: 8 corners, 12 edges, and translate_z

  
A cube is 8 corner points (the list vs in cube.js) joined by 12 edges (the list fs). The code projects only the corners. Then it draws a straight line between the two projected corners of every edge. Before projecting, translate_z pushes the cube forward by dz.

4 · Spinning the cube: rotate_xz

  
rotate_xz(p, θ) turns a point around the vertical y axis by the angle θ (the Greek letter theta). The point keeps its height y; only x and z change. The code turns the corners before translate_z, while the cube's center is still at (0, 0, 0), so the cube spins around its own center. The picture shows the corners after translate_z too (with dz = 1), so the cube sits in front of E.

5 · Everything together: one frame of the code

  
Every frame, frame() grows the angle, clears the canvas, and sends both corners of every edge through four functions. Then it draws a line between the two results. How this page draws its 3D pictures

  
There is no 3D library on this page. For every dot and line, the page measures how far the point is to the right of its own camera, above it, and in front of it, then divides the first two numbers by the third — the same x ÷ z, y ÷ z as above. Dragging a picture only moves that camera.
